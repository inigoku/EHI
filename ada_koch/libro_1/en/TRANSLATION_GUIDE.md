# Translation guide — *Los Amantes de la Espiral* → *The Lovers of the Spiral*

Target: US English, commercial romance for women (Kindle), literary but warm. Close first person.
The Spanish is the authority. Translate everything; never summarize, cut, merge or add.

## Fidelity rules
- One paragraph in, one paragraph out. Keep every blank line, every `---` scene break, every heading.
  The paragraph count of your output must equal the paragraph count of your input.
- Keep the rhythm: short sentences, fragments, one-line paragraphs, repetitions. Do not smooth fragments
  into full sentences, do not join paragraphs, do not explain anything the Spanish leaves implicit.
- Keep italics (`*...*`) exactly where they are (thoughts, messages, foreign words, titles).
- Keep metric units (meters, kilometers, degrees Celsius). Spell numbers as the Spanish does.
- Avoid filler tics that the author has pruned: don't introduce "like someone who", "suddenly",
  "slowly", "I stood there", "I didn't know what to say" where the Spanish doesn't have them.

## Dialogue
- Spanish rayas → English double quotes, with standard US punctuation.
  `—No soy su primo —dijo Iter.` → `"I'm not her cousin," Iter said.`
  `—Mira. —Señaló el cielo—. Ahí.` → `"Look." He pointed at the sky. "There."`
- A paragraph that is only a line of dialogue stays a single paragraph.

## Headings
- `# Primer acto · El faro` → `# Act One · The Lighthouse`
- `# Segundo acto · Iter` → `# Act Two · Iter`
- `# Tercer acto · El nombre` → `# Act Three · The Name`
- `## 7 · Ada` → `## 7 · Ada` (same). `## 30 · Julian` stays.
- `### Maruxa · Stuttgart, abril de 1966` → `### Maruxa · Stuttgart, April 1966`
- `### Maruxa · Costa da Morte, hoy` → `### Maruxa · Costa da Morte, Today`
- `### 23 · Ada (continúa)` → `### 23 · Ada (continued)`
- `**FIN DEL LIBRO I**` → `**END OF BOOK I**`; *La historia de Ada Koch continúa en el Libro II.* → *Ada Koch's story continues in Book II.*

## Names and fixed terms
- Ada Koch, Iter, Julian (no accent), Maruxa, Klaus, Brais, Lucía, Lola, Fátima, Álvaro Lasheras Gil, Marcos, Lúa (the dog, she). Keep all Spanish accents in names.
- *el de las pinzas* (Brais before he is named; he jump-started her car with *pinzas*) → **the jumper-cables man**. His joke "Me dedico a tener pinzas" → "Having jumper cables is what I do."
- *la abuela / mi abuela*: in Ada's narration "my grandmother"; as a form of address "Abuela"; when Iter/Julian narrates and uses it as her name, "Abuela" (capitalized, no article).
- *percebe(s)* → goose barnacles; *percebeiro* → barnacle fisherman.
- *la furgoneta del pan* → the bread van. *la furgoneta* (Brais's) → the van.
- *la mujer verde* (Lucía in a green face mask) → the green woman.
- *la de Ramiro* → Ramiro's wife.
- *el faro* → the lighthouse; *la galería* → the gallery (the iron balcony round the lantern); *la linterna* → the lantern; *la torre* → the tower.
- *el cuaderno* (Ada's, where she draws) → the sketchbook, echoing the series title; Klaus's *cuaderno de hule* → the oilcloth notebook. *el baúl* → the trunk. *la goma* → the eraser. *la contraventana* → the shutter.
- *Cuentos de Ada* → *Ada's Stories*. *El niño que se queda* → *The Boy Who Stays*.
- *dibujitos* (Álvaro's belittling word) → "little drawings"; "Tú y tus dibujitos" → "You and your little drawings."
- "Aaada" (Álvaro's drawn-out way of saying her name) → keep "Aaada".
- *el cuartel* (de Cee) → the Guardia Civil post (in Cee); *el guardia* → the officer.
- *el modelo europeo*, *iteraciones* → the European model, iterations / ensemble members (keep "iteration" where the name *Iter* is being explained).
- Cloud names in Latin, German words (*Mutter*, *Schäfchenwolken*), Portuguese (*Um, dois, três*, *Outra vez*) stay as they are.
- Place names stay: Costa da Morte, Cee, Corcubión, Fisterra, Vigo, Stuttgart, Neckar, calle Ibiza → Calle Ibiza, Madrid, A Coruña.
- Food: empanada, caldo (broth), pulpo (octopus) — keep empanada in Spanish.

## Galician and the grandmother's voice
- Galician lines stay in Galician, in italics if they are in italics, and the Spanish gloss that follows (if any) is translated.
  e.g. `—Cóidama, alemán.` / `Cuídamela.` → `"Cóidama, alemán."` / `Look after her for me.`
- Endearments stay: *miña rula* (and the gloss "Mi palomita" → "My little dove"), *rula*, *filla*, *nena*.
- *o alemán* stays.
- "Non durmo ben desde que morreu Klaus. Seis anos." stays in Galician.

## Format of your output
Plain Markdown, same structure as the input. No front matter, no translator notes, no commentary.
