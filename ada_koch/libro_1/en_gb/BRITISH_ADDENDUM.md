# Addendum to the Orkney bible — no trace of Spain

The author's rule, overriding anything in ORKNEY_BIBLE.md that contradicts it:
**no reference to Galicia or Spain may remain. Nobody reading the English novel may suspect the original is Spanish.**
Germany stays (Klaus, Stuttgart, his mother, the German words). Portugal may stay (Fátima). Everything Spanish or
Galician becomes Orcadian / Scottish / British. The voices, the beats, the tense rules and British English stay as they are.

## The family, now Orcadian

- **Maruxa → Maisie.** Born in Stromness. Her official name at the factory is **Mary** ("Fräulein Mary"); her full name is **Mary Isbister Rendall**. *Maruxiña* → *peedie Maisie*. Klaus's mispronunciation *Marusha* → *Meisie*. Her little sister's name for her, *Tata* → *Sissie*.
- Her father is a Stromness fisherman; her mother sings the old island songs (*the auld sangs*, *the auld sang*). Her uncle is away on the Aberdeen trawlers (not Newfoundland).
- **Why she goes to Stuttgart (March 1966)**: the herring has failed, the new engine for her father's boat is still owed, and an advert in *The Orcadian* says a Stuttgart firm wants girls with steady hands for its assembly line. She is one of a handful of island girls who answer it. Departure: the *St Ola* from Stromness to Scrabster, her mother on the pier, then the long train south — the Highlands, Glasgow, England flat as a table, the Channel, France — to Stuttgart. Replace the Vigo platform and the girls from Ourense/Lugo with the Stromness pier and two or three girls from Caithness and Lewis on the train.
- **In Stuttgart** (headings stay "Stuttgart, …"): the workers' hostel (*Wohnheim*) is full of Italian, Greek, Portuguese and Turkish girls. **Pilar from Lugo → Pina, from Calabria** (Italian; same round face, same big hands, same chorizo → *salame* in newspaper, same sweetheart waiting). **Fátima stays Portuguese**, husband in Lisbon, silent, teaching Klaus the *pasodoble* in the laundry is fine — keep "Um, dois, três".
- **The Galician Centre → the Italian club** (a basement smelling of octopus, black tobacco and grappa); the men of the Lugo table → the Calabrian table; **Manolo → Gino** at the bar. Klaus, drunk on grappa (the bible's *peatreek* only for Orkney home-brew), sings Maisie's mother's auld sang there; the Italians cry with laughter.
- **Ramón from Ourense → Rab, a Shetlander** on the line, who speaks broad island Scots to her, talks of home (the simmer dim, Up Helly Aa, his mother posting him bannocks wrapped in *The Shetland Times*). His Galician lines become Shetland/Orcadian Scots.
- **Klaus's mother** objects to her as *Ausländerin* and *Fabrikarbeiterin* (drop *Spanierin* and *katholisch*: Maisie is Church of Scotland).
- **Lake Constance** stays (it is in Germany). The **Neckar** ring stays.
- **1970–71**: Klaus's map of Galicia with pencil circles → **a map of Orkney** with circles round Stromness, Stenness, Orphir, the Hoy villages. The letter from her sister (mother's heart) calls her home to **Stromness**. Headings "Costa da Morte, May 1971" etc. → **"Stromness, May 1971"**, "Stromness, Summer 1971", "Stromness, September 1971".
- Her father's boat engine breaks down in **Hoy Sound** (not the *ría*); Klaus fixes it on the pier; her father calls him *the German* for thirty years, "the way you'd say *my ain boy*".
- **The wedding** (September 1971): in the Stromness kirk; the old minister takes three weeks to agree to marry a Lutheran (no bishop, no holy card). Her mother in a wheelchair with her psalm book (not the Virgen del Carmen card). Reception in the hotel bar after the fish supper (not *pulpo*). She sings her mother's auld sang.
- **Afterwards** they simply stay in Stromness. Remove the Flotta bridge as a reason for coming (they were already there); if Klaus's work is mentioned, he mends boat engines on the pier and later works at the Flotta terminal.
- Klaus learns **Orcadian** on the Stromness pier in 1971 with his oilcloth notebook (*Krivvens* with a k, etc. — already in place).
- Klaus's earlier notes in Maisie's language were in Spanish; now they are in **English**, misspelled the German way (*Hart. Rane. Hoap.*; *Wat do dey kol you at hom?*). "He wrote a word in German, I wrote it in English."

## Ada, now British

- Ada grew up in **Edinburgh**. Her father left for **Aberdeen** when she was nine. The surname paragraph: Koch is her mother's name; her mother changed Ada's by **deed poll** that winter, and her father signed without a word, "the way he signed everything that took him further from us". No double surnames, no registry.
- She lived three years in **London** with the ex. **Madrid → London** everywhere (the flat in Calle Ibiza → **a flat in Kentish Town**; "the man from Madrid" → "the man from London"; the conference stays in Kirkwall).
- **Álvaro Lasheras Gil → Alistair James Hargreaves** (at the police station she says the full name). He still drags her name: *Aaada*.
- **Lucía → Lucy** (London; the green face mask; "the towel guy"). **Marcos → Marcus**.
- Her client company is in **Aberdeen**, not Bilbao. Her mother rings on Sundays from Edinburgh.
- **Abuela → Granny** (address and name; in Iter/Julian's narration, "Granny" as her name). *Café con leche* → milky coffee. Euros → pounds; a UK mobile number has eleven digits.
- Any "Spanish" (language, accent, food, "a soft Spanish j") → English/Scottish equivalents. Iter's first words and jokes adjust to English.
