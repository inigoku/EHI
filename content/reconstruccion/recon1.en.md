---
title: I. THE ARCHITECTURE THAT EMERGES
section: RECONSTRUCTION
---

## NARRATIVA

The soap stretched between my hands as if it had a memory of its own. In that minimal, almost unnoticed gesture, the curve it formed left a hollow in the center. It was a newborn space that did not exist a second before.

The morning light entered through the window and stopped right there, in that tiny concavity. It seemed to recognize something I still did not know how to name, which is why I stood looking at it: I did not fully understand why it claimed me.

I washed my hands and left the soap in the soap dish. I went out into the street with the feeling that this hollow was still with me: it was not in my hands, it was in my chest, like a concavity asking to be accompanied. The air had the texture of things that are about to change, not abruptly, but with that minimal displacement that occurs when a door opens half a centimeter and lets in a smell you did not expect.

I walked toward the port. The streets were damp and seemed to preserve the memory of the night, as if the city had spent hours remembering something I had forgotten. I have always thought that cities remember better than people: they keep the curves, the cracks, and the corners where someone stopped to think. That morning every paving stone seemed to have something to say.

Upon reaching the pier, the water receded a few centimeters, just enough for the oar to touch bottom. The blow traveled up my arm and settled in my wrist; it did not hurt, but it warned. The water breathed: it was not absence, it was a different form of presence, a gesture asking for attention.

I stood still with the oar resting on the damp sand. The water seemed to move with an intention that did not belong to the sea, but to something deeper. I looked at the horizon, not the sea's but the other one, the one that shifts inside the skin. I felt something in me slide a few millimeters inward: it was a soft contraction that was neither sadness nor fear, but a silent warning.

I walked again and passed by the square where the fountain no longer remains. The city seemed to remember something I had forgotten. The paving stones had a strange shine, as if a memory that was not mine had polished them, and in that shine I recognized the same persistence I had seen in the hollow of the soap.

There are streets that no longer exist, but I keep walking them in dreams. I walk them as if they were mine, even though I know they no longer belong to anyone. The lost city breathes in me with the calm of rust: it advances without haste and takes possession of its own iron. Time does the same; it does not destroy, it rewrites.

Sometimes my hand trembles, not from fear but from an accumulation of horizons, as if the gesture of holding a glass were too large for a single day. That tremor is a form of memory: the body remembers even when the mind does not know what to remember.

That morning, as I walked, I felt the hollow of the soap was still with me. It was not an image, it was a soft pressure that accompanied me. I stopped in front of an old door whose wood was marked by time, by water, and by sun. The rust advanced without haste over the lock: it transformed without destroying.

I thought of the hollow of the soap, of the blow of the oar, of the lost city, and of the trembling hand. Then I understood: the hollow does not appear to be filled, it appears to be seen, and in that vision an architecture begins. It is not built. It emerges.

## ENSAYO

The hollow is not absence. It is a form of pressure. A concavity that pushes outward so that something can enter. It is not void, even if it seems so. It is a space that claims to be accompanied, not filled. That is why it stays in the chest even when it is no longer in the hands.

Spinoza, in the Ethics, calls *conatus* the effort to persevere in being. But what perseveres in the hollow? Not the matter, which has withdrawn. Not the form, which is negative. Something more difficult: the *tendency* to be accompanied. The hollow is not passive; it is a structural demand. Just as the event horizon of a black hole is not a surface but a condition—the condition that information, once crossed, can no longer return without ceasing to be what it was—the inner hollow is not a lack but a living frontier.

The blow of the oar was not an accident. It was a signal. The water receded a few centimeters and revealed the bottom, as if it wanted to remind me that every withdrawal is also a revelation. The void is not empty: it is full of warnings we do not know how to read. Every minimal variation of the world is a form of language, a phrase we do not understand but which affects us.

The inner horizon moved without noise. It was not a tremor, nor a contraction, nor a dramatic gesture. It was a soft displacement, almost imperceptible, but enough for the body to notice it. The horizon is not a line: it is a living frontier that responds to breath, to exhaustion, to fear, to waiting. When the horizon moves, it is not the world that changes: it is us.

The lost city appeared as it always appears: without warning, without logic, without geography. There are streets that no longer exist, but which keep being walked in dreams because memory does not obey the map. Memory obeys matter. The curve of the soap. The blow of the oar. The trembling of the hand. The withdrawal of the water. The light that stops in a minimal concavity. The lost city is time's way of reminding us that not everything that disappears is gone.

The rust advanced over the lock of the old door. It did not destroy: it transformed. Rust is not deterioration, it is persistence. It is time taking possession of its own iron. I like to think that the hollow works the same way: it does not erase, it rewrites. Every hollow is a form of time, a mark that cannot be eliminated because it is not a wound, but a structure.

The hand trembles from an accumulation of horizons. It does not tremble from fear. It trembles because it has held too many things that could break. It trembles because it has accompanied hollows without knowing it. It trembles because it has touched unexpected bottoms. It trembles because it has felt withdrawals and returns. The hand remembers even when the mind does not know what to remember.

The hollow does not appear to be filled. It appears to be seen. And in that vision begins an architecture that is not built, but emerges. An architecture made of concavities, of withdrawals, of horizons that move, of cities that persist, of rusts that advance, of hands that tremble, of waters that breathe.

The hollow is a form of company. It asks for nothing. It demands nothing. It only wants to be accompanied. And when we accompany it, something in us is ordered, even if we do not know what. The architecture of the hollow is not an external structure: it is a way of being in the world. A way of looking. A way of listening. A way of waiting.

The hollow is the place where the book begins.

## CIERRE

The wind changed when I stood up from the bench. It was not an abrupt change, but enough for the skin to notice it before the mind. The sea remained still, as if keeping something it did not yet want to show. I walked a few steps and felt the hollow in my chest adjust to the movement, as if accompanying every gesture without delay.

I stopped at the end of the pier. The air had a different density, as if it were about to break or open. I did not know which of the two it was. Sometimes the world prepares for something without saying it, and one can only wait for it to happen.

I took a deep breath. The hollow responded. Not with pain. Not with pressure. With a clarity it did not have before. It was as if the body had found a place to be, a place that did not depend on the city, nor the sea, nor time, nor rust. A place that depended only on the shape of the hollow.

I stood there a moment, without moving. The inner horizon settled. It did not contract. It did not expand. It just stayed. And in that staying, something was ordered.

It was not a revelation. It was not an answer. It was not an end. It was a beginning.

A beginning that asked for nothing.  
A beginning that demanded nothing.  
A beginning that only wanted to be accompanied.

The hollow breathed.  
And I breathed with it.
