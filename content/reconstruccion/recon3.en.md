---
title: III. THE WEIGHT OF LIGHT
section: RECONSTRUCTION
---

## NARRATIVA

The morning began with a different light. It was neither stronger nor weaker, but it had a weight I had not felt before. The light did not only illuminate: it occupied space. It leaned on surfaces as if it needed to rest. I stood for a moment observing it, trying to understand what had changed.

On the table, the glass I had left the night before had a reflection I did not remember. It was not a shine. It was a density. The light remained trapped on the upper edge, as if the glass were holding it. I ran my finger along the edge and felt a minimal vibration, almost imperceptible, but present. The light was not still. It breathed.

I went out to the street. The air had a clarity that was not transparency, but presence. The light leaned on the walls, on the cars, on the leaves of the trees. It did not limit itself to illuminating: it touched. And in that touching, it left a mark.

I walked toward the square. The paving stones had a shine that did not come from the sun, but from the light itself. It was as if the light had decided to stay there, on the surface, without advancing. I stopped and observed how a shadow formed beside me. It was not my shadow. It was the shadow of the light.

On the corner bench there was an older man. I had not seen him before. He had his hands resting on a cane and looked toward the ground, as if the ground were telling him something. I approached slowly. I did not want to interrupt whatever it was he was listening to.

When I was beside him, he lifted his head. His eyes had the same weight as the light. They were not tired eyes. They were eyes that had seen too much. He looked at me for an instant and then looked back at the ground.

"The light weighs," he said.

He did not say it like someone explaining something. He said it like someone remembering something. Like someone who has lived something that cannot be forgotten.

I sat beside him. I did not know what to answer. I did not know if I should answer. The light kept leaning on the paving stones, on the bench, on the cane, on the man's hands. It was a light that did not want to leave.

"There are days when the light weighs more," he added.

It was not a poetic phrase. It was an exact phrase. A phrase that described something that was happening. The light had weight. And that weight was felt in the body.

The man lifted his cane and pointed to a crack in the ground. The light accumulated there, in the crack, as if the crack were a vessel.

"The light seeks hollows," he said.

The phrase pierced me. Not because it was profound, but because it was true. The light sought hollows. Just like water. Just like memory. Just like the form that returns.

I kept looking at the crack. The light settled into it, as if the crack were a safe place. A place where it could rest without disappearing.

The man stood up. He said nothing more. He walked slowly toward the exit of the square. The light accompanied him. It did not follow him. It accompanied him.

I stayed there a moment, observing how the light withdrew from the crack and returned to occupy the air. It was not an abrupt withdrawal. It was a gentle withdrawal, like that of water on the pier. A withdrawal that left a form.

I took a deep breath. The light responded. Not with shine. Not with heat. With weight.

And in that weight, something held.

## ENSAYO

Light is not only clarity. Light is matter. Not in the physical sense, but in the structural sense. Light occupies space. Light leans. Light rests. Light weighs.

The man with the cane explained nothing. He argued nothing. He justified nothing. He only spoke the truth: the light weighs. And that truth needs no demonstration. It is felt. It is recognized. It is accompanied.

The light seeks hollows. Not because it wants to hide, but because hollows are places where the light can remain without being interrupted. The light does not fill the hollows. It inhabits them. It accompanies them. It sustains them.

In physics, light is both wave and particle. But there is a third way to understand light: as *structural memory*. The light that weighs is not the light that illuminates objects; it is the light that remembers having illuminated them. It is the luminous trace that remains when the source has gone. It is the density that persists on the edge of the glass, in the crack in the ground, in the concavity of the soap.

The hollow of the soap, the withdrawal of the water, the form that returns, the crack in the glass, the mark of the glass, the presence of my mother on the corner: all of that is light. Not visible light. Structural light. Light that reveals. Light that remembers. Light that insists.

The light weighs because memory weighs. The light weighs because absence weighs. The light weighs because form weighs. The light weighs because time weighs.

Light is an architecture. Not an architecture that is built, but an architecture that emerges. An architecture made of hollows, of withdrawals, of cracks, of concavities, of forms that return, of presences that do not leave.

Light is the place where the third part of the book begins.

## POEMA

The light weighs.  
  
It does not illuminate.  
  
It leans.  
  
It rests.  
  
It breathes.  
  
The light seeks hollows.  
  
It finds cracks.  
  
It inhabits concavities.  
  
It stays  
where the world  
withdraws.  
  
The light remembers  
what matter  
cannot say.  
  
The light sustains  
what time  
lets fall.  
  
The light weighs.  
  
And I weigh with it.

## CIERRE

The light changed when I stood up from the bench. It became neither stronger nor weaker. It became more precise. More present. More mine.

I walked toward the exit of the square. The light accompanied me. It did not follow me. It did not guide me. It accompanied me.

The hollow in my chest adjusted to the movement. The light settled into it. Not to fill it. To sustain it.

The light weighs.  
And I weigh with it.
