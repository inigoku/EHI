---
title: V. THE RUPTURE THAT INSISTS
section: RECONSTRUCTION
---

## NARRATIVA

I could not sleep. Not because of noise. Not because of light. Not because of fear. Because of something worse: because of a feeling that everything was about to shatter.

I stood up suddenly. The chair fell to the floor. I did not pick it up. I did not want to touch anything. I did not want anything to touch me.

The air was dense. Not like before. Not tense. Not expectant. Dense. As if there were something suspended in it. Something that should not fall. Something that, if it fell, would split me in two.

I opened the window. The impact was violent. The frame trembled. The glass vibrated. I did too.

The street was empty. Too empty. As if everyone had decided to disappear at once. As if the city had been evacuated without warning me. As if I were the last to know. As always.

I walked toward the plaza. Every step was a blow. Every blow was a warning. Every warning was a threat.

At the fountain, a man was sitting. He did not move. He did not breathe. He did not blink. He was just there, like a badly placed statue.

I approached. Too fast. Too close. Too aggressive.

"What are you doing here?" I spat.

The man lifted his head. His eyes were two hollows. Not empty. Not dead. Hollows. Hollows that wanted to fill themselves with something. With me.

I stepped back. The man advanced. He did not walk. He advanced. As if the air pushed him. As if the plaza claimed him. As if I were what he needed.

"No," I said. I do not know who I said it to. To him. To the plaza. To the night. To me.

The man extended his hand. Not to touch me. To take something. Something that I had. Something that I was.

I ran. I did not think. I did not look back. I did not breathe. I ran as if running were the only thing that could prevent the world from breaking.

But the world was already broken. I was too.

## ENSAYO

The rupture is not aggression. The rupture is reorganization. A deep, structural, inevitable reorganization.

When a system tenses too much, when a structure stretches beyond its limit, when a form can no longer sustain its own weight, the rupture appears.

The rupture does not destroy. The rupture redistributes. It redistributes forces, memories, presences, absences.

The man at the fountain is not a threat. He is a symptom. A symptom of a system trying to rearrange itself. A symptom of a structure seeking a new equilibrium. Just as the singularity of a black hole is not a point of destruction but a limit where known laws cease to apply, the internal rupture is not a collapse but a threshold.

Aggressiveness is not violence. It is a signal. A signal that the body is trying to defend a form that no longer belongs to it. A signal that consciousness is trying to sustain a state that no longer exists.

The rupture is not an end. The rupture is a transit. A transit toward a form we do not yet know. A transit toward an architecture we cannot yet name.

But the transit hurts. And the pain is not a metaphor.

## POEMA

The rupture insists.  
  
It does not warn.  
  
It does not wait.  
  
It does not forgive.  
  
The rupture opens  
what I wanted  
to keep closed.  
  
The plaza breathes  
with a new hollow.  
  
The man advances  
with an old absence.  
  
I run  
because I do not know  
how to sustain  
what is breaking.  
  
The rupture insists.  
  
And I cry  
because I do not know  
whether I should stop it  
or let it advance.

## CIERRE

The night did not retreat. It stayed. It settled. It arranged itself in every corner, in every hollow, in every part of me that did not yet know it was broken.

I walked toward home. The rupture advanced with me. It did not follow me. It did not guide me. It passed through me.

The hollow in my chest adjusted to the movement. The rupture settled into it. Not to destroy it. To reorganize it.

The rupture insists.  
And I insist with it.
