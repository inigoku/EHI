---
title: II. THE FORM THAT RETURNS
section: RECONSTRUCTION
---

## NARRATIVA

The day began without intention. There were no signs, no warnings, no gestures to announce anything. Only a soft clarity entering through the window, a clarity that did not entirely illuminate but did reveal the contours of things. I got up slowly, as if the body needed to remember how it is done.

In the kitchen, the glass had a watermark on the rim. It was not a large mark, nor deep, nor significant. It was a minimal curve, a trace left over from the night before. I ran my finger along it and felt that the texture was different from the rest of the glass: colder, more precise, more present. The mark said nothing, but neither was it silent.

I thought of the hollow from the previous chapter. Not as a memory, but as a continuity. The hollow was still there, not in the hands, not in the chest, but in the way I looked at things. Everything had a concavity, a withdrawal, a memory. Even the glass.

I went out into the street. The air had a smell I could not identify. It was not sea, it was not earth, it was not rain. It was a smell that seemed to come from a place that does not exist, but that insists. I walked toward the main avenue. The shops were closed. The metal shutters had rust stains that advanced without haste, as always.

On the corner, I saw my mother.

She was not there. She could not be there. But I saw her. Not as an apparition, nor as a memory, nor as a desire. I saw her as one sees something that belongs to the world even though it is no longer in it. She was standing, in her light coat and her small purse, looking toward the bus stop as if waiting for something that never arrived.

I stopped. I did not want to approach. I did not want to speak to her. I did not want to break the form in which she was there. The presence had a precision that should not be touched. My mother looked toward the street as if the street were a book she knew by heart. She did not move. She did not breathe. She did not change. But she was there.

A car passed and the image dissolved. It did not disappear: it rearranged itself. My mother was no longer on the corner, but she remained in the air, in the light, in the way the avenue curved toward the plaza. I kept walking without knowing if I had seen her or if I had remembered her.

I reached the bus stop. The glass had a fine crack, almost invisible. The crack advanced diagonally, as if searching for a point it had not yet found. I ran my hand along the edge and felt that the crack had the same texture as the mark on the glass, as the hollow of the soap, as the withdrawal of the water. A form that returns.

I sat on the bench. The bench was cold. The cold always tells the truth. I looked at the street and thought of my mother. Not of her absence. Not of her death. I thought of her form. Of the way she held things. Of the way she looked before speaking. Of the way she left a hollow between sentence and sentence so that the world could enter.

The bus arrived. I did not get on. I did not want to go anywhere. I only wanted to be there, at the stop, with the crack, with the light, with the form that had returned without asking permission.

I took a deep breath. The hollow responded. Not with pain. Not with nostalgia. With a clarity it did not have before.

And in that clarity, something moved.

## ENSAYO

The form that returns is not a recollection. It is not memory. It is not an apparition. It is a persistence. An insistence. A way the world has of saying: "This is still here, even if you do not see it."

My mother appeared on the corner, but she did not appear as a ghost appears nor as a thought appears. She appeared as a form appears that never left. The people we love do not disappear: they change places. They cease to be in the body and begin to be in matter. In the light. In the curve of a glass. In the crack of a pane. In the withdrawal of water. In the concavity of soap.

Bergson, in *Matter and Memory*, distinguishes between habit-memory and image-memory. But there is a third memory, which is neither habit nor image: texture-memory. The memory that is not represented, but felt. The memory that does not remember a face, but a way of being in the world. That memory is not in the mind: it is in matter. And matter, against all expectation, remembers.

The form that returns is not an image. It is a structure. An architecture that sustains itself in absence, but does not depend on it. Absence does not erase. Absence reveals. Absence is a kind of presence that does not occupy space, but occupies time.

My mother was on the corner because the corner remembered her form. Corners remember. Streets remember. Benches remember. Matter remembers even when it seems soft. It does not remember with images. It remembers with textures. With hollows. With cracks. With withdrawals.

The form that returns is not a voluntary act. It is not something one seeks. It is something that appears when the world decides to show it. The form returns because the world has memory. And that memory is not linear. It is not logical. It is not human. It is material.

The form that returns is a form of company. It asks for nothing. It demands nothing. It only presents itself. And when it presents itself, something in us is ordered. Not because we understand, but because we recognize.

The form that returns is the place where the second part of the book begins.

## POEMA

My mother appeared  
on the corner.  

She was not there.  

But she appeared.  

As appears  
the form  
that never left.  

The light held her  
an instant.  

The air remembered her.  

The street  
opened a hollow  
so she could be.  

She did not speak.  

She did not look.  

She did not breathe.  

But she was there.  

As is  
the mark on the glass.  

As is  
the crack in the pane.  

As is  
the withdrawal of the water.  

As is  
the minimal concavity  
of the soap.  

My mother appeared  
because matter  
remembers.  

Even when it seems  
to remember nothing.  

The form returned.  

And I returned with it.

## CIERRE

The bus left. The street recovered its rhythm. The light changed its angle. The corner became a corner again. But something had remained. Not an image. Not a memory. A form.

The form that returns does not stay. It does not settle. It does not fix itself. It only passes. And in that passing, it leaves a mark. A mark that cannot be erased because it is not in the skin, nor in the mind, nor in time. It is in matter.

I got up from the bench. The hollow in my chest adjusted to the movement. I walked a few steps and felt that the form was still with me, not as a presence, not as an absence, but as a structure.

The form returned.  
And I returned with it.
