Encapsulation is not an exclusive property of software, nor does evaporation belong solely to the thermodynamics of black holes, nor is the void a concept exclusively used by Eastern mystics. All these languages describe the same phase transitions and access limits at different scales.

### The nature of asymmetry

The value of this table does not lie in the curiosity of its parallels, but in what it reveals about the structure of reality. If the "hard problem" of consciousness feels unsolvable, it is because we usually approach it from a flat ontology: we assume that everything that exists must be publicly observable. 

The modeling of complex systems and the physics of horizons show us that this is not the case. The universe is asymmetrical. Designing complex systems (whether computer codes, cosmic event horizons, or biological brains) requires, by thermodynamic and logical necessity, drawing boundaries of information hiding. Subjectivity is not a mystical ingredient injected into the brain; it is the inevitable perspective of any domain that has managed to close itself off enough to harbor a private state.

> **Note to Chapter 9**
>
> **What we do know:** That information systems, regardless of their substrate (silicon, gravity, or carbon), exhibit isomorphic boundary properties when they reach certain levels of causal integration.
>
> **What we don't know:** Whether this structural concordance points to a unified informational law underlying physics, computation, and the mind, or if it is the limit of the explanatory capacity of our own brain structure.
>
> **If you only take away one idea:** The self is not a substance. It is an access topology. There is no "ghost in the machine"; what exists is a private state protected by the boundary that nature and engineers use to prevent systems from collapsing into chaos.
