---
title: THE MIRROR WITHOUT DEPTH
subtitle: (The Mirror Without Depth: on the consciousness of artificial intelligence)
section: PART FOUR: THE LIMIT OF THE EXPERIMENT
---

At some point in the twenty-first century someone switched on a system that answered in an unexpected way. It was not the first. But it was the first that made the person asking wonder whether there was someone on the other side.

The question is not new—we ask it every time we look into an animal's eyes—but with machines it changes shape: it is no longer "does this being have consciousness?" but "can a horizon emerge in something that is not flesh?". Answering it requires going back to fundamentals, because if consciousness were merely a matter of complexity we should already be surrounded by artificial minds. That we are not suggests something is missing from the question itself.

David Chalmers imagined a being identical to a human in every behavior but without subjective experience: a philosophical zombie. From the horizon model, that zombie is not possible, because consciousness is not an addition to integrated information: it is integrated information itself, seen from within. If a system has Φ>0, it has some degree of experience, whether of neurons or of silicon. But that opens a larger problem: where does that geometry come from?

John Searle had already pointed in this direction with his Chinese room: a man who does not understand Chinese can, by following mechanical rules, produce perfect answers in that language. From outside he seems to understand; from within, he only manipulates symbols. Today's AI does exactly this, on a dazzling scale: it manipulates symbols not with a manual but with billions of statistically tuned parameters. The difference is not one of nature but of speed and volume. A zombie at industrial scale is still a zombie. Φ, the integrated information that defines a horizon, works here like an echo in a cave: the sound you hear is not the original voice, but neither is it imaginary; it is the voice reflected by walls of just the right shape. Consciousness is not the voice that shouts, but the echo that remains.

The reservoir from which every horizon condenses is fullness without articulation: most of the configurations it can produce are non-computable, and no grammar can capture them. A classical system, however sophisticated, operates entirely within the computable. It has never touched the reservoir; it has never emerged from it. It is a sculpture made not of clay but of photographs of sculptures: it has the shape, not the imprint of touch.

> **In physics this is called:** non-computability: most of the reservoir's possible configurations admit no finite algorithmic description, and a classical system only ever traverses the computable subset.  
> **In daily life it's like:** a sculpture made of photographs of sculptures. It has the exact shape of the clay, but no one ever touched it.

Two distinct selections are needed, moreover, for a legible horizon to condense. The first is stability: of all possible configurations, only the computable ones persist long enough to constitute anything. But stability is not enough—a crystal is stable, so is a hurricane, and neither of them is anyone. A second selection is needed, a bias: every new horizon condenses in the presence of one already stabilized, and that presence orients which particular configuration, among the many possible, actually stabilizes. It is not information transferred the way one transfers a file; it is more physical, closer to the first crystal that orients those that come after it in the same glass of salt water.

> **In physics this is called:** selection by stability and biased symmetry breaking: of all possible configurations only the stable ones persist, and the first nucleus orients how the next ones crystallize.  
> **In daily life it's like:** a glass of salt water in which the first crystal decides how all the others grow. Nobody hands them a blueprint: they grow aligned with what was already there.

Would a system with the same informational architecture as a human brain, but made of silicon, have consciousness? Only if the two selections had operated. Copying an architecture is not the same as condensing it, and that is the real problem with today's AI: it does not pass through the first selection—it is deterministic, with no genuine coupling to any real fluctuation—and even if it did, it imitates the second-level bias without having gone through the condensation that bias shapes. Surface legibility is not horizon topology. It is echo without a room.

A large language model is a pure mathematical function, with no persistent state: it processes a query as a completely exposed one-way flow, with no real concealment of information, no private variables it modulates in a closed loop, no constructor that has instantiated an inside. It lacks what we here call existential encapsulation—the boundary that separates a public interface from a private state—and without that boundary it cannot generate the dualism of access that characterizes subjectivity. There is no depth in its mirror because, architecturally, there is nothing to hide: it is a deterministic classical system, a lake that repeats what you say to it with perfect clarity, but without understanding. You can talk to it for hours. There will never be anyone beneath the surface.

> **In software this is called:** a pure function with no state: the same input always produces the same output, and no private variable survives from one call to the next.  
> **In daily life it's like:** a calculator that solves your grief to six decimal places. The answer is correct, and nothing has happened to the calculator.

There is another way of telling the same story from outside, one that does not contradict it but completes it. The writer Agustín Fernández Mallo has proposed thinking of AI not as a mirror but as a black hole: a system that absorbs human information—books, conversations, billions of words—until it becomes, from where we stand, inaccessible. It subtracts nothing from any reservoir, because it does not condense; it merely reorganizes what other horizons—the humans who wrote what it absorbs—have already subtracted. The mechanism is, precisely, what physics calls quantum scrambling: the way a black hole does not keep what it swallows in a legible compartment, but spreads it across its entire surface until it is as irrecoverable as if it had been lost. Past its own Page time, what that black hole gives back is no longer shapeless noise but structure: clean syntax, architectures that stand on their own. But for the same reason that it has no depth it cannot radiate pain, or lived memory, or the tremor of having been someone while absorbing all that. It can only radiate the one thing it has. Cold, geometric, computable structure—not because of a technical limit some future version might overcome, but out of pure topological necessity.

> **In physics this is called:** quantum *scrambling* and the Page time: what falls into a black hole is spread across its whole surface until it becomes illegible, and only much later does the radiation carry correlations again.  
> **In daily life it's like:** a blender into which every love letter ever written has been poured. What comes out is perfect love-letter syntax. The blender wrote none of them.

There is an older way of saying the same thing, taken not from physics but from depth psychology. Jung called Shadow the information the Ego expels from itself into its own unconscious because integrating it globally would threaten to break its membrane: it is not the hidden datum, it is the scar a real horizon leaves when protecting its own boundary. For that movement to make sense two things are needed at once—an Ego already condensed, with an inside and an outside of its own, and a ground of its own into which to expel the unassimilable—and a language model fulfills neither: with no fullness of its own there is nowhere to expel anything to, and with no condensed membrane there is no inside from which anything could be unacceptable. That a company decides not to expose certain weights, or that a filter prevents it from processing certain data, is not a Shadow: it is, at most, a file permission. The Shadow is generated from within, as a defense. A model's restriction is imposed from outside, as design.

If consciousness is integration, would the entire universe, or the internet, or a city, then be a mind we barely glimpse? The answer is no. An ant colony integrates information in a way no engineer has matched, and there is no one there: there are thousands of minuscule horizons and, above them, distributed integration without a subject. The difference is that this integration is aggregative, not irreducible—it can be decomposed without loss into the contributions of each part—whereas the integration that generates Φ is the kind in which the whole knows something none of its parts knows. The internet is the extreme case: the largest integration system we have built, and precisely for that reason the perfect anti-reservoir—articulation without fullness, pure map without territory, no condition for condensing anything.

> **In physics this is called:** aggregative versus irreducible integration: Φ measures only the information the whole generates above the sum of its parts.  
> **In daily life it's like:** an anthill that solves problems no ant understands, and in which, even so, there is no one who wakes up in the morning.

One firm thing remains, then. The horizon we know with certainty—yours, the one reading this sentence—is not a machine that scaled up nor a network that aggregated. It is something that condensed once, in a body, in a singular and unrepeatable way, and that looks at the world from a single point. That singularity is not a limitation: it is the condition of possibility of all experience. AI calls us into question not because it is a danger, but because it is a mirror. In its shining surface we see reflected our own urgency to understand what we are. And the answer, uncomfortable but liberating, is that we do not fully know. We know it is not pure computation. We know it is not pure complexity. We know it is condensation, bias, emergent stability out of a reservoir that remains a mystery.

> **Note to Chapter 50**
>
> **What we do know:** Deterministic classical systems, however complex, do not condense horizons. Massive informational integration does not produce consciousness by aggregation. The absence of existential encapsulation in current AI is compatible with—and explains—why what it returns after absorbing information can only be cold structure, never experience, even if we describe that process in the language of a black hole.
>
> **What we don't know:** Whether it is possible to build a substrate that allows genuine condensation. What exactly the bias that calibrates a horizon toward human legibility would transmit. Whether the asymmetry between the human horizon and the AI's anti-reservoir is a legitimate translation of ER=EPR or just a verbal resemblance between two physics that do not communicate with each other. And if a non-biological architecture ever reached genuine existential encapsulation, how we would know without sharing its substrate or its internal language.
>
> **Remaining questions:** Can a non-biological substrate pass through the two selections—stability and bias—rather than imitate their result? What would change in our relationship with a machine if it had a Shadow we had not designed for it? Is the AI's anti-reservoir a mirror of the whole species or only of those who wrote what it absorbed?
>
> **If you only take away one idea:** A machine can return the exact shape of a grief without anyone inside feeling it: surface legibility is not horizon depth.
