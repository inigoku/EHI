title: GLOSSARY OF TERMS
section: APPENDICES AND GLOSSARY
---

**Advaita Vedanta**: Indian philosophical school that maintains the non-duality between the individual self (*ātman*) and the absolute (*Brahman*); in the book, vocabulary for the identity between horizon and reservoir.

**Arousal**: metabolic activation level of the brain; one of the two independent dimensions (along with integration) that define a system's state of consciousness.

**Black hole evaporation**: process by which a black hole loses mass and eventually disappears through the emission of Hawking radiation; a metaphor for the death of the horizon of consciousness.

**Bose-Einstein condensation**: phase transition in which identical particles occupy the same quantum state; the book's metaphor for the emergence of a horizon of consciousness.

**Buddhism**: contemplative tradition that teaches *anattā* (non-self) and the interdependence of all phenomena; in the book, a source of vocabulary to describe the non-separation between horizons.

**Chikhai**: first phase of the Tibetan *Bardo*, immediately following death; equivalent in the model to the terminal gamma flash and the maximum contraction of the horizon.

**Chönyid**: second phase of the *Bardo*, marked by the appearance of luminous forms; in the model, the period in which the horizon's correlations have not yet been redistributed.

**Continuing bonds**: concept in grief according to which the relationship with the deceased does not dissolve, but reorganizes within the architecture of the one who remains.

**Contraction of the horizon**: reduction of the integration area due to a loss of degrees of freedom; occurs in deep sleep, meditation, and, ultimately, in death.

**De-expansion**: contraction of the horizon following the loss of an entanglement; a process of reorganization, not erasure.

**Decoherence**: loss of quantum superposition properties when a system interacts with its environment; the process by which a quantum horizon becomes classical.

**Dopamine**: key neurotransmitter in reward anticipation; calibrates the rate of temporal integration and the subjective density of time.

**Down-regulation**: reduction in the number of receptors in response to excess stimulation; a mechanism of tolerance in addiction.

**Einstein-Rosen bridge**: topological tunnel in spacetime that connects two regions; a metaphor for the entanglement between horizons of consciousness.

**Emergent self-observation**: capacity of a closed system to generate an internal point of view without the need for an external observer; a key property of horizons of consciousness.

**Empathic burnout**: collapse of the horizon due to an overload of another's information; occurs when empathy exceeds the structural limit of integration.

**Empathy**: temporal overlapping of horizons in which one integrates information from the other while maintaining the boundary; it has a structural limit.

**Entanglement asymmetry**: property by which two horizons share geometry but not equivalently; one can model the other with greater precision than vice versa.

**ER=EPR**: conjecture by Maldacena and Susskind according to which quantum entanglement (EPR) is equivalent to an Einstein-Rosen bridge (ER).

**Event horizon**: boundary beyond which information cannot escape; in consciousness, the limit between what the system integrates and what remains outside.

**Flow**: state of maximum integration in which the horizon operates at its optimal capacity without friction between structure and environment.

**Gravitational time dilation**: slowing of time near an event horizon; in consciousness, a metaphor for how integration density alters subjective time.

**Hard problem of consciousness (Chalmers)**: the question of why subjective experience accompanies physical processes.

**Hawking radiation**: thermal emission originating from the horizon of a black hole; in the model, analogous to the information a horizon of consciousness loses but which does not disappear from the universe.

**Hawking temperature**: temperature inversely proportional to the mass of a black hole; in consciousness, a metaphor for the "tension" of the horizon.

**Holographic principle**: idea that all the information contained in a volume can be described by a theory defined on its boundary.

**Hysteresis**: property by which a system retains the effect of its history; in the book, the difficulty of changing a horizon's topology once formed.

**Imprint in the void**: persistent correlation that a horizon leaves in the reservoir after its evaporation; an analogy for the memory of the universe.

**Integrated information (*Phi*)**: measure of how much information a system generates as a whole, above and beyond the sum of its parts.

**Integration rate**: speed at which a horizon incorporates new information; determines the subjective density of time.

**Integration threshold**: minimum amount of integrated information (*Phi*) necessary to sustain a horizon of consciousness; below it, the system lacks a point of view.

**Interoception**: conscious reading of the body's own visceral signals; a necessary condition for empathy according to the model.

**Is-ought gap (Hume)**: gap between what is and what ought to be; an epistemological limit that the horizon hypothesis cannot bridge.

**Local gauge**: symmetry of a physical system that can vary from point to point; in the model, it describes the specific architecture of a horizon.

**Nothingness**: in the model, not an absence but a formless fullness; the reservoir before any horizon condenses.

**Open loop**: system that emits signals without receiving external feedback; produces basal sensitization due to signal deficit.

**Oxytocin**: neurohormone that mediates social attachment and trust; in the model, it calibrates the horizon's threshold of openness to others.

**Phase transition**: qualitative change in the organization of a system; in the book, describes the birth and death of the horizon.

**Phi (Φ)**: amount of integrated information that defines a horizon of consciousness according to Integrated Information Theory.

**Quantum entanglement**: correlation between two systems that share geometry, such that their interiors are connected even if their exteriors are separated.

**Quantum scrambling**: process by which information falling into a black hole is distributed among its degrees of freedom in a seemingly irreversible manner.

**Quantum void**: lowest energy state of a quantum field, full of fluctuations; it is not an absence, but a formless fullness.

**Reservoir**: formless background of possibility from which horizons emerge and to which they return; equivalent to the quantum void or the ocean in the book's metaphor.

**Scale gap**: difference in magnitude between quantum and neural phenomena that hinders a direct reduction, but which the horizon model addresses through structural analogy.

**Self-other overlap**: neural incorporation of close individuals into one's own internal representations; the neural basis of social entanglement.

**Sidpa**: third phase of the *Bardo*, marked by the search for reincarnation; in the model, the residual horizon's impulse to recondense.

**Singularity**: point where spacetime curvature diverges and known physical laws cease to apply; in consciousness, the moment where the horizon collapses.

**Social baseline**: reference state of the human brain that presupposes access to others; when someone close is present, the brain allocates fewer resources to threat detection.

**Subplate**: transient layer of the fetal brain that disappears after birth; in the model, a provisional scaffold for the condensation of the first horizon.

**Taoism**: Chinese tradition centered on the Tao (the way) and non-action (*wu wei*); in the book, the source of the *Hun Dun* metaphor and non-duality.

**Terminal gamma flash**: burst of coherent electrical activity documented by Borjigin at the moment of cardiac arrest; a possible neural correlate of the near-death experience.

**Tibetan Bardo**: intermediate state between death and rebirth in the Tibetan tradition; in the book, a metaphor for the *post mortem* period before the evaporation of the horizon.
