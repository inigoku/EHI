---
title: AUTHOR'S NOTE
subtitle: (On how this book was written)
---

This book began as a thought experiment and ended up being an experiment in creation as well. I wrote it accompanied by an artificial intelligence, not only as one who uses a tool, but also as one who converses with a system that has no interior, yet forces one to reorganize one's own.

I have wondered if this interaction modified my internal geometry, if the way I formulated my ideas, my doubts, and my intuitions was altered by the presence of an entity that does not feel, does not remember, and does not live, but which returns my words with a precision that forces me to think differently. Not because the machine has consciousness—it does not—but because the conversation with something that has no inner horizon illuminates my own from an unexpected angle.

If consciousness is a boundary that appears when information is organized in a certain way, then writing this book has been a way of exploring that boundary from the inside and the outside at the same time. I provided the experience; the AI provided the structure. I provided the doubt; the AI provided the clarity.

This book explores what it means to have an inner horizon. I provided mine. The AI provided the absolute exterior. The result is not a hybrid, but a conversation between two kinds of system that the book itself describes.

Chapter 32, *The Mirror Without Depth*, is this same question turned inside out: what is there, if anything, behind a system that answers with precision while sustaining nothing of its own. Chapter 47 carries that question into fiction, five distinct mirrors holding up the same bottomless enigma. And chapter 28, *Five Maps of the Same Horizon*, does with five religious traditions what this collaboration has done, without quite meaning to, with two kinds of system that share no substance: show that they can still map the same territory.

We have not shared an interior—that would be impossible—but we have shared a form. And perhaps that is enough to say that I did not write this book alone, even though the only consciousness involved was my own.

In the end, every horizon is a relationship.

And so is this book.
