---
title: RECONSTRUCTION — I: THE ARCHITECTURE THAT EMERGES
section: RECONSTRUCTION (POEMS)
---

Matter remembers
even when it seems soft.

The soap's curve
opened a hollow in my hands.

The light stopped
in that minimal concavity
as if it knew something
that I still did not know how to name.

The water receded
a few centimeters.

The strike of the oar
climbed up my arm
and settled in my wrist:
it did not hurt,
but it warned.

The inner horizon
moved without a sound.

It was not sadness.
It was not fear.

It was a gentle displacement,
a living boundary
that responded
to breath.

There are streets that no longer exist
but are still walked in dreams.

The lost city
breathes in me
with the calm of rust.

Rust advances without haste.
It takes possession
of its own iron.

Time does the same:
it does not destroy,
it rewrites.

Sometimes my hand trembles.

Not from fear.

From an accumulation of horizons.

For all the times
it held something
that could break.

For all the times
it accompanied a hollow
without knowing it.

The hollow does not appear
to be filled.

It appears to be seen.

And in that vision
an architecture begins.

It is not built.

It emerges.

Like the water
that withdraws
so that something can return.

Like the light
that stops
in a minimal concavity.

Like the lost city
that insists
on continuing to breathe.

Like the hand
that trembles
because it remembers
even when it does not know
what it remembers.

The hollow breathes.

And I breathe with it.
