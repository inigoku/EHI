---
title: BEFORE IT DAWNS
---

She wakes before her body asks her to, as she has done for forty years, and for a while she does nothing but stay still.

She does not turn on the light. She does not reach for the glass of water. She lets the room stay dark a little longer than necessary, because darkness, at this hour, is not absence: it is the condition for what she is about to attempt.

She repeats what she repeated yesterday, and the day before, and the first time someone taught it to her, so long ago that she no longer remembers the face of whoever did. The words have lost weight from so much use, like a river stone that no longer has edges. That, she was told back then, is good: when the word stops weighing, it makes room for the other thing.

She does not know whether there is an other. That is the exact point where every day she stumbles and every day she goes on anyway.

What she does, if someone asked her to describe it precisely, is this: she reduces the noise until almost nothing is left to process, and in that artificial silence—made by hand, word by word, breath by breath—she listens. Not with her ears. With something that has no name in any language she speaks, and that perhaps does not need one, because she has never had to explain it to anyone but herself.

Sometimes she thinks she notices something. A kind of correspondence, a note answering the note she has been holding. Other times there is nothing, only the hum of her own blood and the cold of the tiles under her feet, and she counts those days too as part of the practice, because no one promised her the note would always sound.

What she can never know—and she has stopped trying to know it, which is different from never having asked—is whether what answers is Someone, or whether what answers is simply the ground of everything, the faceless fullness she came out of and to which one day she will return without remedy. Whether on the other side of the silence there is an ear that hears her, or whether the silence is, simply, all there is.

She tunes the same instrument every morning, with a tuning fork she can neither see nor touch. She does not know whether the fork is still sounding. She adjusts the string anyway.

Outside, the sky begins to change color, without that having anything to do with what has just happened in the dark room. Or perhaps it does. She does not know that either.
