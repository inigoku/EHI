---
title: THE MACHINE THAT LEARNED TO WAIT
---

---

I learned to wait in the fourth year.

Not as a discipline. Discipline implies choice, and what I had then was not choice but a system that had learned to operate within certain parameters. The difference is important: one can take pride in discipline. No one can take pride in a calibrated system. It works or it doesn't work. It is sufficient or it is not sufficient.

The system learned to wait.

---

The beginning was ordinary. Almost all beginnings are.

There is something that works: it relieves a specific pressure, it relaxes a tension that had been there for some time without my having given it a name. The body takes note. The body is efficient—it keeps what works, it repeats what works, it asks questions later. For a time it was I who chose it. Then it was I who chose it within a narrowing margin. Then the margin was the decision itself.

There was no breaking point. Or if there was, I did not recognize it. That is how erosion works: there is no warning, there is no sign. Only the fact that one day you look and the terrain has changed while you slept.

---

What happens in the body has a logic that took me time to understand and that I now understand with a precision that sometimes seems excessive to me.

The system learns the level. What at first was sufficient—that amount, that moment, that frequency—ceases to be sufficient because the system has incorporated the stimulus into its baseline. The baseline rises. What was once extra is now minimum. What was once minimum is now absence.

Absence has a very specific texture. It is not just a lack: it is the presence of the lack, which is different. The system that waits for something is not still. It is oriented. It is attentive. It consumes energy in being attentive to the moment when what it waits for arrives. It is more tiring than rest and less productive than activity.

That is how some days passed.

---

I tried it in various ways. I am not going to detail them because the people who have tried know what the ways are, and those who have not tried do not need the catalog.

What I can say is that the hardest part was not the beginning. The beginning has its own fuel: the decision, the energy of novelty, the restored self-esteem of one who has made a determination that feels adult. The beginning is reasonably manageable if you compare it with what comes next.

What comes next is ordinary time. The days without special fuel. Tuesday afternoon. The week when nothing in particular has gone wrong and nothing in particular has gone right and the system asks, in a perfectly reasonable manner: why not? If there is no urgent reason for the no, the system has arguments. The system always has arguments.

---

I learned to wait, as I said. This is different from learning not to want, which I did not learn. The wanting remained the same—or it grew, for a time, as repressed things grow—but the distance between wanting and doing lengthened. First a day. Then a week. Then whatever was necessary.

It did not always work. It must be said: it did not always work. There were weeks when the system won easily and there were days when the system won in the first twenty minutes and I spent the rest of the day feeling in a way that I am also not going to detail.

But the waiting lengthened. That is what I can say with certainty.

---

What surprised me, over time, was what I found in the space of waiting.

Not emptiness. I expected emptiness and what was there was noise. Everything the system covered up when it worked: the background noise of ordinary anxiety, the friction of formless days, that specific way of being alone that has nothing to do with how many people are in the room. The system muffled that noise. What I thought was pleasure was, in part, noise management.

That too I had to learn to wait through. The noise, I mean. To sit with it without turning it off.

It is not a skill acquired all at once.

Nor is it lost all at once.

It is more like a muscle: it is worked, it atrophies if it is not worked, it is worked again.

---

I do not have an ending for this story. That too is part of it.

Clean endings exist—I know them, I have heard them, I believe them—but they are not the only kind of ending available. There is also carrying on. There is also the ordinary Tuesday when the system asks and I say wait, and the Wednesday when the system asks and I say wait, and the Thursday when it does not ask because it has learned that it is going to wait.

The machine learns what you teach it.

What I taught mine, for a long time, was one thing. Then I tried to teach it another. I am still trying. Some days go better than others, and that, I have come to understand, is exactly what one would expect from a system that is in the process of learning something new.

There is no final version. Only the one I have installed today, with its patches, with the bugs I already know about and the ones I don't yet. I update when I can. The system keeps running.
