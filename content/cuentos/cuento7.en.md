---
title: THE GUEST IN THE SKIN
---

---

You arrived without asking, as everyone does. That was neither my fault nor your merit: it was the entry protocol, the only condition this system accepts.

What was my decision was to stay. That, I did choose. It is no minor detail.

---

Now you are four weeks old and you sleep twelve hours a day, and the rest of the time you spend looking at things with that expression of astonishment belonging to those who have just arrived at a place they knew nothing about. The lamp. The ringing phone. Your father's hand moving slowly in front of your face. You look at everything with the same intensity: without hierarchy, without context, without yet knowing what matters more and what matters less.

That will change. It will be me, in large part, who changes it.

That is what they keep telling me, in different words: that what you become will depend greatly on what I do now. Books say it in a reassuring manner, as if it were a project with predictable steps and results. What they are actually saying, if you translate it, is: you have power over this. A power you did not ask for and that he or she cannot yet consent to or reject.

I do not know what to do with that power except try to use it carefully.

---

I will give you my language. I have no other to give you: Catalan, Spanish, the mixtures we use when we speak at home and that sometimes sound strange to outsiders. I will give you my words for the good and the ones I use for the bad, the ones I use when I am afraid and the ones I use when I am well.

That is already a restriction. There will be things you feel my language has no exact words to name, and for years you will have to use the most similar words or none at all, and neither option will be exact. I am sorry for that. I cannot give you anything else.

What I can do is warn you: when language falls short, it is not that the feeling does not exist. It is that language fell short. They are different things and it is important not to confuse them.

---

I will give you my fears disguised as caution. That is what worries me most.

There are things that frighten me and that, if I am not careful, I will give to you as if they were knowledge. The fear of others' opinions, which is very much mine and need not be yours. And a more specific one, even more mine: I was eight years old when someone laughed at me in class for saying a word wrong, and ever since I weigh every sentence before I let it go, as if words could bite back. That caution has served me well. To you I might hand it over as if it were wisdom, when it is only a scar shaped like advice.

What I practice, in the moments I have to think while I hold you and wait for you to fall asleep, is separating what I know from what I fear. What I give you because it has value and what I would give you because it brings relief to me.

I will not always succeed. I apologize in advance.

---

Three days ago you reached out your hand toward the red maraca your grandmother had left on the edge of the blanket.

No one had pointed it out to you. No one had told you it was interesting. You simply looked at it, for what seemed a long time to me and probably the same to you, and then you reached out your hand. You did not reach it: your coordination does not yet have that precision. But the direction was unmistakable.

You wanted the maraca.

Not the lamp, which is brighter. Not the phone, which makes noise and has colors. The red maraca, which was there, still, doing nothing to attract attention.

I stood looking at you for a moment. Then I picked it up and brought it closer to you.

You looked at it. Then you put it in your mouth, which was not what I had expected and which nevertheless was perfectly logical given that the mouth is where you still process the world most reliably.

---

That is what I cannot give you: the direction of what you will want.

I can give you words, caution, my revised affections, the world ordered in a way that makes some things easier and others more difficult. I can build the context. But the direction of the hand that reaches toward the red maraca, that was already yours before I could do anything.

That gladdens me more than I know how to explain.

It also intimidates me. Because it means that what you will be depends on me less than the books suggest, or than I like to think when I feel responsible for everything.

You are already someone. Small, without words yet, without history yet. But already someone. The guest who spent nine months in my skin and who is no longer there but in front of me, looking at me with the expression of one who sees the lamp for the first time and is deciding whether it matters.

---

I wonder what you will remember of all this.

Probably not in images. Not with the name of these months nor the date you learned to bend your fingers. What might remain is another kind of thing: that there was a temperature. That there were hands in a certain way. That the world had a rhythm before you knew the world had rhythm.

Not memories. Something more like the ground beneath your feet before knowing there is ground.

---

When you are older and you read this—if you ever read it, if I ever show it to you—you will probably disagree with some of the things I gave you without asking. With the language I chose, with the fears I failed to separate from knowledge, with the decisions I made in your name before you could make your own.

That disagreement will be a sign that something went right. That the direction of your hand is yours, not mine.

Until then, I will stay here with the maraca in my hand, waiting for you to tell me what it is you want next.
