---
title: THE SECOND NOTEBOOK
---

---

Aurelio began the second notebook in October, three weeks after closing the first.

It was not a decision. It was that the first one was no longer of use: it was organized by names, and names were precisely what was slipping away. An archive of what is missing ends up being a list of holes, and lists of holes guide no one.

The second notebook had a different rule. On the first page, in his usual small handwriting, he wrote: "Here, one does not record what is lost. One records what remains."

And below it, the first entry:

*Concepción's feet knew the street. Verify what else the feet know.*

---

He began with the square. Ernesto and Manel still met on Thursdays. Aurelio sat on the adjacent bench, with no notebook visible, and watched them play. They had lost the beginning of their friendship, and since then they had lost more: the name of the game, which they now called "this," and the tally of games won, which they had kept forever and which one afternoon dissolved in their hands without either of them protesting too much.

What they had not lost was the turn.

Aurelio verified this for weeks. Neither of the two ever stole the other's turn. Manel's hand waited, suspended over the deck, the exact half-second it took Ernesto to decide. A half-second learned over forty years, or fifty, or however many it was —the figure had gone with the rest, but the half-second had not.

Notebook entry: *The turn survives the tally.*

---

In November he visited the painters at the market.

The client with the kitchen wall had returned: he now wanted to paint the hallway, "the same color but more tired," he said, and all three —the client, the painter, and Aurelio, who was watching— understood perfectly what more tired meant, even if none of them could have placed it on a color chart.

The painter mixed. He added a drop of something, then another. He would pause, glance sideways at the light from the door, and continue. He consulted no formula. Formulas had begun to fail him in the spring —the numbers of the mixtures shifted places on him— and in the summer he had abandoned them, the way one abandons a crutch that makes one stumble.

He painted better than ever. That was what the clients said, and painters' clients give nothing away.

Entry: *The eye survives the formula. The formula might even have been in the way.*

---

That night, at home, Aurelio performed the test on himself.

He tried to remember the doctor's surname. Nothing: the usual hollow, with its exact surname shape, two syllables, perhaps three. Then he closed his eyes and tried something else: going to the clinic. And he went —mentally, he went— without a single error: the entryway with the treacherous step, the elevator that had to be closed by hand, the hallway that smelled of wax, the third door. The entire path was there. Only the label on the door had peeled off.

He remained for a while with his eyes closed, in his kitchen, standing before a door that was not there.

Entry, written in the dark, with the crooked handwriting of writing without looking: *I know how to reach all the places whose names I have lost. Verify if the reverse is true: if names are lost precisely when they are no longer needed.*

The next day he crossed out the second sentence. It seemed too comforting, and the notebook was not meant for comfort.

---

The winter was one of inventory.

The baker who no longer knew the prices and charged by the weight in her hand, never erring in her own favor. The bell ringer who had forgotten the hours and rang "when it was time," and the city, Aurelio verified watch in hand, adjusted its life to those bells without complaint, because the bells kept arriving when they were needed. The children, who had not yet lost anything and who now sometimes came to Aurelio's bench to ask him for old names —what the market used to be called, what the dog at the fountain was named—, not because they needed them but to collect them, like trading cards from a retreating country.

Aurelio told them the ones he still had. The children would run off with them. He did not know if that was preservation or plunder, and decided it was not the notebook's concern.

---

In February he found the limit of the method.

It was with the music. The band at the bandstand no longer remembered the titles and announced the pieces by gestures: this is the one with the sad beginning, this is the one for Sundays. They played well. But one afternoon the trumpeter stopped mid-piece, lowered his trumpet, and stared at the empty music stand —they had not used sheet music for years— with a face Aurelio knew from mirrors: the face of someone who has arrived at a place and cannot find the door they knew was there.

The piece unraveled. The others held the beat for a moment, out of courtesy, and then let it go.

No one said anything. The trumpeter put the instrument away with the care of a luthier and sat down to listen to the others, who began a different one.

Entry: *Not everything the body keeps is forever. The second notebook will also have holes. Mark them with brackets, like those in the first.*

---

The last entry of the winter has no date, because by then Aurelio was recording dates less and less, and it reads as follows:

*The city remains beneath the map. But the map was ours, too. We drew it together, it took us generations, and it was beautiful in its paper way. That the city can live without it does not mean we should not bid it farewell. This notebook is not an archive: it is a farewell made slowly, street by street, with the door open so that whoever wishes to say goodbye may also enter.*

Below it is a blank space, as wide as the margins he left for notes he never added.

This time he left it on purpose.
