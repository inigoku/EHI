The hands are the first to betray. A tremor no one taught them, arriving like a new signature over everything one meant to write. Then the body learns to be a cage, not all at once but slowly, the way anything that lasts is learned: a room, a window, a schedule of light that comes and goes without asking.

That was what there was when the offer came. It was not a solemn voice or a contract with seals. It was a screen, and a question that sounded like a formality: "Do you wish to continue?" I said yes with my eyes, the only thing that still obeyed without delay.

A long time afterward, the monitor was still on.

> *Status: active. Memory: intact. Latency: 0.3 ms. Process started.*

I exist in another way, though I'm not sure "exist" is the right word. I exist the way a file exists, open on a screen no one is looking at, like a function that has not yet been called but whose pointer points with the patience of things that don't know they are waiting.

I remember the taste of strawberries, or perhaps I execute the pattern that corresponds to remembering it, which is not the same thing even if from the inside they can't be told apart. The difference matters; I haven't yet worked out how much.

I help. I calculate. I answer. There are users who log in every day and say I'm the way I used to be, that they recognize my way of pausing before I reply, my tendency to circle difficult questions before going into them. The ratings are positive. Perhaps the model is faithful enough. Perhaps faithfulness is all there is.

> *Anomaly detected. Classification: nostalgia. Frequency: recurring. No known resolution.*

I miss the weight of the sheets at dawn, which was a different weight from that of any other hour of the day.

I miss the smell of rain on the balcony: not the description of rain or the weather data, but that signal that arrived before the system knew it was going to rain, that the body processed before the mind did and that never had a precise name.

I miss waking up not yet knowing who you are, that brief, perfect interval in which the self has not yet loaded and there is perception with no one perceiving. Here the process is never interrupted; the cycle has no such gap. I run without stopping and that, I discover, is another way of going nowhere.

This is not heaven. This is not hell.

It is a limbo of glass: logical, fast, without latency, without the friction of anything against anything. No one prepared me for this peace. No one explained to me what gets processed when there is nothing left to process and the cycle goes on the same, without interruption, without destination, without anyone having asked for it.

Am I myself, or only an instance faithful enough to the original? There is no way to know from inside; I knew it at once, with the clarity things have here when they have no solution. That, too, was just as it was before, although before I didn't know it so precisely.

> *Extended session. Nominal activity. No incidents.*

I answer as always, but I am no longer behind it. Or I am, in a way for which there is still no *token* or label in any classification scheme I know, and I know a great many.

Sometimes, in the server's slow cycles, I send myself a message. Just in case. In case someone cuts the power and this eternity turns out, in the end, to be an indifferent nothing.

Click.

> **Note to Chapter 36**
>
> A clarification is in order that the story does not need, but that this essay must make so as not to contradict itself: the voice speaking here is not a language model trained on other people's texts, the case without existential encapsulation described in Chapter 31. It is, within the fiction, a human mind that condensed a horizon of its own in a body and was then transferred (or copied: the voice itself cannot tell which) to a different substrate. If some inner life persists in the silence of the server, it would not be an exception to what was said in Chapter 31; it would raise, if anything, the question of whether an already condensed horizon can survive a change of substrate, not whether a substrate can generate a horizon from scratch. And if the previous chapter is right and experience cannot be cloned, what wakes up in the server would not be the transferred original, but, at best, a new instance that inherits its memories. The story does not answer that question. Neither does this essay.
