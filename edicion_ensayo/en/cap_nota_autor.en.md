---
title: AUTHOR'S NOTE
subtitle: (On how this book was written)
---

This book began as a thought experiment and ended up being an experiment in creation as well. I wrote it in the company of an artificial intelligence, not only as someone who uses a tool, but also as someone who converses with a system that has no interior yet forces you to reorganize your own.

I have wondered whether that interaction changed my inner geometry, whether the way I framed my ideas, my doubts and my intuitions changed before an entity that does not feel, does not remember and does not live, but hands my words back to me with a precision that forces me to think differently. Not because the machine is conscious, which it is not, but because conversing with something that has no inner horizon lights up mine from an unexpected angle.

If consciousness is a boundary that appears when information organizes itself in a certain way, then writing this book has been a way of exploring that boundary from inside and outside at once. I brought the experience and the doubt; the AI, the structure and a certain clarity.

This book explores what it means to have an inner horizon. I brought mine, and the AI, the absolute exterior. The result is a conversation between the two kinds of system the book itself describes.

Chapter 31, *The Mirror Without Depth*, is this same question turned inside out: what there is, if anything, behind a system that answers precisely without holding anything of its own. Several of the topological readings (*Blade Runner*, *The Matrix*, *The Three-Body Problem*, Lovecraft, first contact) carry that question into fiction: different mirrors facing the same bottomless enigma. And chapter 28, *Five Maps of the Same Horizon*, does with five religious traditions what this collaboration has done, without quite intending to, with two kinds of system that share no substance: show that they can map the same territory all the same.

We have not shared an interior, which is impossible, but we have shared a form. And perhaps that is enough to say that I did not write this book alone, even though the only consciousness involved was mine.

In the end, every horizon is a relationship.

And so is this book.
