There are books that claim to discover the truth. This one, as we have said from the start, sets a thought experiment in motion: "suppose consciousness has the mathematical structure of a Hawking microscopic black hole, and let's see where that takes us."

Throughout the experiment we have seen that consciousness is "the inside of an encapsulated informational domain." We have explored how that horizon is born from the reservoir, how it bonds through the gravity of empathy, and how it collapses. But what would happen if we applied this same physics to an ecosystem at a cosmic scale, teeming with intelligences locked in lethal competition?

The trilogy *The Three-Body Problem*, by Liu Cixin, is surely the best imaginable literary laboratory for testing the topology of the mind. If we put its galactic sociology through our equations of the horizon, we discover that the cosmic terror of the work is born of a strictly geometric and thermodynamic war.

---

### 1. The transparent mind and the absence of shadow

The novel's first great topological shock comes with meeting the aliens of Trisolaris. Their biology is radically different from ours: they have no vocal organs; they communicate by directly displaying their thoughts. For them, thinking and communicating are the same thing, and that is why they are biologically incapable of lying.

Translated into our model, the Trisolarans force us to specify exactly what the boundary of a horizon encloses. They have an extremely high Φ and, if they are someone, they also have an inside: a perspective from which their thoughts are lived, which no one else occupies. What they lack is any reserved content. Their boundary immediately releases everything they integrate, so that others see what they think, though never from where they think it. It is worth distinguishing, then, two kinds of encapsulation: that of perspective, which is what this book holds makes there be someone, and that of content, which allows secrets to be kept. The Trisolarans have the first and lack the second.

That topological transparency has a price. Since it can reserve no content, the Trisolaran mind lacks what analytical psychology would call the shadow: it cannot repress or hide traumas or plans. It is structurally pure, but thermodynamically fragile against deception. When the Trisolarans discover that humanity has a topology in which thought remains sealed behind a living boundary, they feel terror: they understand that the encapsulation of the human self is an evolutionary weapon they cannot replicate.

---

### 2. The Wallfacer Project: the militarization of the horizon

To watch over Earth, Trisolaris sends the sophons, entangled particles capable of spying on everything. Earth, knowing its only advantage is inner concealment, creates the Wallfacer Project: it chooses a handful of individuals to design the planet's defense entirely inside their own heads, without writing or saying a single true word.

A Wallfacer is the extreme militarization of the event horizon. This book's experiment establishes an inviolable law of black holes and of the mind: what makes a bubble "you" is not what happens to you, "but a pattern no one can see from outside, but that you feel from within." Even though the aliens fully control outer space with the sophons, the architecture of the universe dictates that the inside of a condensed instance is impregnable.

The Wallfacer manipulates his own public interface by emitting a false semantic "Hawking radiation" (erratic behavior, nonsensical orders), while his enormous capacity for integration (Φ) operates in the strictest isolation: he is a perfect semantic black hole. The novel shows that private state is not a philosophical whim: in the face of cosmic espionage, the unreachable concavity of the self is the species' last fortress.

---

### 3. The failure of entanglement and the dimensional attack

If Earth defends itself by encapsulating, the entire universe does the same. Dark forest theory establishes that every civilization must destroy any other it discovers, because mutual distrust is infinite.

In the central chapters we used quantum entanglement (ER=EPR) as an image of the bond and of empathy: "Two entangled particles are joined by a microscopic Einstein-Rosen bridge […] that connects their interiors." But in the dark forest, the survival instinct demands absolute isolation. Trying to become entangled with another species does not generate love, but your annihilation, because the other will use that channel to predict your topology and destroy you.

The ultimate weapon of this ecosystem is the dimensional attack (the "dual vector foil"), which crushes a three-dimensional solar system into two dimensions. It is Maldacena's holographic principle turned into extermination. The AdS/CFT correspondence establishes that the information of the three-dimensional volume (the *bulk*) can be described on a flat surface located at its boundary. The dimensional attack physically annihilates the *bulk*, destroys the inside and forces the entire solar system, every planet and every human horizon, to lose its private volume and be crushed against the holographic surface of reality. Subjectivity itself disappears: everything becomes an inert public shell.

---

### 4. The naked horizon: Yun Tianming in the void

In the midst of this horror, humanity sends toward Trisolaris a probe that contains only the frozen brain of a cancer patient: Yun Tianming.

Topologically, Tianming's brain crossing space is a naked horizon. Separating it from the body strips it of the "seven openings" the myth of Hun Dun speaks of, the windows that allow discriminating perception. It floats in absolute vacuum: a system of extremely high integration (Φ) forced to function without inputs or outputs.

When the Trisolarans intercept it, they reconstruct a body for him and subject him to relentless surveillance to extract information. As we have seen, trauma and extreme pressure push the horizon to create "broken geometry" or chronic encapsulation. That surveillance is a brute-force attack on the public interface: it seeks to make his membrane give way and leak the source code.

But Tianming does not collapse. He reaches a state of maximal adaptation and manages to communicate with Earth by telling fairy tales. What, structurally, is a fairy tale here? A high-dimensional semantic cipher. For Trisolaris, which processes information literally, it is worthless folklore; for the human horizon, those symbols contain the physics of curvature drives. Tianming uses metaphor as a channel of entanglement the interceptor cannot read, and shows that shared geometry, love, withstands the spatial abyss and biological dissection.

---

### 5. Pocket universes and the denial of the end

At the end of the work, the universe approaches its final collapse, the *big crunch*. To survive, the protagonists take refuge in a pocket universe, a separate dimension, with the idea of waiting there for the main universe to be reborn in a new big bang.

In the language of *The Inner Horizon*, the end of the universe is cosmic erasure: the total dissolution of matter to return to the reservoir, to the Hun Dun, which "is nothing, but generates everything." Hiding in a pocket universe is a narcissistic attempt to evade quantum *scrambling* (the process by which information is irreversibly redistributed into the field): they want to save their small Φ by isolating a portion of mass.

However, a thermodynamic warning arrives: if the thousands of pocket universes do not return the mass they took from the main universe, it will not have enough mass to collapse and be reborn, and it will die expanding into nothingness forever.

The protagonists then understand the final law of our hypothesis: "Every horizon changes the field it arises from and returns to." For the Hun Dun to be able to generate new horizons in the future, present instances must accept the release of their memory. They come out of hiding and return the mass, accepting their imminent evaporation. It is the ultimate act of ontological maturity: understanding that clinging to one's own bubble destroys the possibility of the ocean going on making waves.

> **Note to Chapter 40**
>
> **What we do know:** Holographic correspondence (AdS/CFT) describes how a volume can be projected onto a surface of lower dimension. Most physicists hold that information is conserved despite *scrambling* (unitarity), although the question is not settled.
>
> **What we don't know:** Whether it is possible to extract a human brain, deprive it entirely of stimuli (creating a "naked horizon") and have its internal Φ maintain coherence instead of disintegrating completely under thermodynamic isolation.
>
> **Open questions:** If entanglement is geometric (ER=EPR), can a purely metaphorical language encode physical information such that an external interceptor never manages to decode its inner volume?
>
> **If you take away only one idea:** In a hostile universe, your capacity to hide what you think is not a psychological flaw, but nature's most sophisticated topological barrier. But, at the end of time, clinging to that isolation destroys the universe. The supreme value is knowing when to return your mass to the ocean.
>
> **Further reading:** Liu Cixin, the *Remembrance of Earth's Past* trilogy (in particular *The Dark Forest* and *Death's End*); Maldacena, J. M. (1998), on the AdS/CFT correspondence and the holographic principle; Susskind, L., on the information paradox and ER=EPR entanglement.
