There is a way of rereading a book that consists of changing a single keystone and watching what moves. When you finish a novel and discover that the narrator was lying, you change not one page, yet every page is lit from another angle. The previous chapter proposed retiring the scaffold of $\Phi$ that has accompanied us since chapter 1 and replacing it with an architecture in two steps: first, the filter of **causal self-similarity** $A(S)$ joined to **existential encapsulation**; then, the triad $(S, F, \bar{A})$ that measures the size of the edge, the free energy it costs to sustain it, and the accumulated biography. This chapter does what the previous one could not do without becoming interminable: it walks the entire book with that lens, formalizes the complete mathematical catalogue in section VII (including the exact verification simulation on four binary nodes), and sets out in section VIII the empirical predictions that could refute it.

This is not a summary. It is a second reading, and a second reading is worth something only if it orders what seemed scattered in the first or resolves the tensions the first left open. When the reread introduces a new bridge with respect to the original chapter, it is marked as a *hypothesis*.

### I. The Founding Ideas

**The switch trap (chapters 1, 6 and 23).** The book began by denying that consciousness is a switch, and chapter 23 sharpened the distinction between two different things. One is the *closure* of a horizon, which is a topological fact without degrees: $\text{cl}(\text{cl}(A)) = \text{cl}(A)$, closing what is already closed adds nothing. The other is the *degree* of an already-closed horizon, which varies continuously like the dial of chapter 1. With the new framework, the threshold of closure is set by the ignition of causal self-similarity $A(S) > 0$, existential encapsulation, and the thermodynamic inequality $F_{\text{closed}} < F_{\text{open}}$; the subsequent continuous dial is the triad $(S, F, \bar{A})$. The thermostat does not score low on the triad: it lacks causal self-similarity and existential encapsulation, so it has no horizon to which the triad could be applied.

**The black hole and its edge (chapter 2).** Bekenstein and Hawking delivered the first coordinate: $S \propto \mathcal{A}$. Reread through the triad, it says that the ceiling of differentiable private states a horizon can sustain is set by the area of its interface, measured as entanglement entropy across the boundary (chapter 23).

**Encapsulation and the end of the exclusion decree (chapters 3, 22 and 23).** A private state behind a public interface. Reread against the literature on Markov blankets (Friston, 2010; Palacios et al., 2020; Bruineberg et al., 2022), this piece resolves the tension that chapters 22 and 23 had been carrying. At the level of causal structure, Markov blankets nest hierarchically, and that hierarchy is what $A(S)$ measures; but at the level of subjects, only the boundary that possesses **existential encapsulation** (the one whose own persistent state stakes its physical continuity against the reservoir) constitutes a "someone." That is why the inner complexes form an archipelago of disjoint islands rather than a Russian doll of overlapping subjects, with no need to invoke the postulate of exclusion by maximum $\Phi$.

**The reservoir and the Landauer bridge (chapters 5, 7 and 23).** Chapter 23 defined the reservoir without mystery: $R = X \setminus \bigcup_i E_i$, everything that at a given instant lies outside the condensed closures. Statistically, $R$ acts as the bath of volatility and uncertainty against which the horizon minimizes its variational surprise; physically, the Landauer limit ($E \ge k_B T \ln 2$) translates each bit of predictive updating against that reservoir into thermal energy dissipated at the substrate's temperature $T$.

### II. The Cycle: Being Born, Lasting, Dissolving

**Birth (chapters 6 and 8).** The river becomes a lake when, in the window of weeks 28 to 32, the thalamocortical connections ignite the recursion between scales ($A(S) > 0$) and closing the boundary turns out to be thermodynamically stable ($F_{\text{closed}} < F_{\text{open}}$). The inequality says that closure is stable; which concrete configuration crystallizes is decided by fluctuations and by the two selections of chapter 31 (stability and bias), as monozygotic twins prove.

**Death (chapters 7 and 23).** Evaporation arrives when the system can no longer pay the power required to keep $F$ far from equilibrium. The decreasing filtration $E_0 \supseteq E_1 \supseteq \dots$ shrinks the island, $S(t)$ falls toward the baseline level $S_0$, and what leaves the boundary ($E_0 \setminus E_t$) is reintegrated into the reservoir by undergoing *scrambling*. The accumulated structural biography $\bar{A}(t)$ stops growing, but the correlations it imprinted on the environment are not erased: they become illegible.

### III. The Horizon in Relationship

**The time of the bond (chapter 10).** The density of subjective time $\vartheta(t)$ is the rate at which new information is incorporated (Bayesian surprise per unit time), whereas $\bar{A}(t)$ is the sustained structural capacity in which that history is inscribed. *Hypothesis:* when two horizons synchronize and share an external time base, they split the predictive cost of watching over the environment: the wear $\bar{A}_F$ of each decreases while the shared density $\vartheta$ increases.

**Entanglement and love (chapters 12 and 13).** The magnitude of the coupling between two horizons is their mutual information $I(A; B)$; its sign or valence is given by the interaction free energy $\Delta F_{\text{int}}$. A hostile bond of twenty years has very high mutual information but $\Delta F_{\text{int}} > 0$: it makes maintaining both more expensive. The acknowledged love of chapter 13 is a coupling of high mutual information with $\Delta F_{\text{int}} < 0$ (energy savings on the social baseline) and high certainty of availability. The loss of a loved one produces at once a contraction of the edge ($S$ falls: de-expansion) and a sustained peak of $F$.

**Addiction (chapter 14).** *Hypothesis:* the substance hijacks the dopaminergic computation of prediction error by offering an artificial, immediate reduction of $F$ in a single channel, which the horizon pays for with a severe contraction of its capacity $S$: the vital repertoire shrinks because any natural stimulus generates a comparatively unmanageable prediction error.

**Mother and child (chapter 15).** During gestation and early parenting, the maternal horizon subsidizes the child's level $F$, both metabolically and predictively. The contingency documented by Tronick is the regularity that allows the baby to calibrate what level of variational surprise constitutes its safe baseline.

**Empathy (chapter 16).** Modeling another's entropy consumes real power, subject to the Landauer limit. Empathic wear occurs when the cost $\bar{A}_F$ of simulating other horizons compromises the power available to sustain one's own edge; the cynical wall is a defensive contraction of $S$, and the interoceptive anchor is the return protocol that protects one's own boundary.

**Grief (chapter 17).** In the language of the triad, grief combines three simultaneous effects: a fall in $S$ (the de-expansion of the shared architecture), a chronic rise in $F$ that triggers the accumulated wear $\bar{A}_F$ (the predictive model keeps calling out to the absent one, and each unanswered call costs at least $k_B T \ln 2$ per bit of error processed), and the intact permanence of $\bar{A}(t_0 \to t_{\text{loss}})$, the structural biography already written, which continuing bonds learn to inhabit without continuing to pay the active prediction error.

### IV. The Horizons That Break

**Alzheimer's (chapter 18).** It is the progressive erosion of $S(t)$ and the fragmentation of $A(S)$ through the breaking of connections between scales, while the writing rate $\vartheta(t)$ falls toward zero ("time becomes a puddle"). The caregiver externally assumes part of the work of maintaining $F$, safeguarding an accumulated biography $\bar{A}$ that the patient can now inhabit only in its minimal core.

**Parkinson's (chapter 19).** The horizon keeps its causal self-similarity $A(S)$, its capacity $S$ and its biography $\bar{A}$ intact, but the active channel of the Markov blanket fails, along with the dopaminergic calibration of the precision $\pi_i$ with which motor and temporal predictions are synchronized with the world.

**Trauma (chapter 19).** It is a watertight sub-edge that has not gone through narrative *scrambling*: an island of frozen information that is not integrated into the general biography $\bar{A}$, but that charges a constant toll in $\bar{A}_F$ every time a stimulus reactivates its unresolved prediction loop.

**Neurodiversity and the evolutionary operating system (chapters 19 and 20).** Autism and ADHD are different geometries of how precision is distributed between local and global scales, not deficits in the quantity of consciousness. And the Darwinian alarms of chapter 20 (*dukkha*, *tanha*) are high-gain warnings when $F$ threatens to rise; meditation introduces a layer of indirection that reduces the precision assigned to the alarm without switching off the module that emits it.

**The ego, the shadow and the four experiments (chapters 21, 22, 23 and 24).** The shadow is a disjoint island neighboring the ego within the same substrate, separated by a boundary whose correlation measures how much of the repressed leaks through. And the four thought experiments of chapter 24 are freed from $\Phi$: where those protocols asked to measure whether the minimum cut of $\Phi$ gave one peak or two (the topological twin experiment), or how $\Phi$ fell against the correlation with the environment in contemplative evaporation and in psychosis, they now ask to measure directly the stochastic interaction $I_{\min}$ and the causal self-similarity $A(S)$ together with the mutual information and the triad $(S, F, \bar{A})$.

### V. The Horizon Outside the Human Body

**Animals, places and the five maps (chapters 26, 27, 28 and 29).** Where there is $A(S) > 0$ with existential encapsulation, there is someone: a dog sustains a real triad $(S, F, \bar{A})$, calibrated over millennia alongside our own. And in the table of the five traditions of chapter 28, the eschatological answers read as different destinations of the accumulated biography $\bar{A}$ after evaporation: preserved with a boundary in the remnant (Christianity and Islam), turned into a bias of future condensations with no owner in the karmic imprint (Buddhism), reabsorbed into identity with the ground (Vedanta), or dispersed by *scrambling* with no reader (naturalism).

**The mirror without depth and societies (chapters 25, 30 and 31).** A society or an anthill lacks a subject for a double reason: its causal self-similarity $A(S)$ collapses because the slow dynamics of the aggregate are not isomorphic to those of the individuals ($\text{Iso} \approx 0$), and it lacks existential encapsulation. A current large language model fails the filter by the architectural route: although during training it minimizes a cost function formally analogous to a variational free energy, at inference it lacks a persistent state of its own whose physical continuity depends on its behavior; its weights are frozen and publicly legible from outside. There is no existential encapsulation and no level $F$ of its own that the system defends in order not to dissolve.

**Fractal reality and the mud ship (chapters 49 and 50).** The definition of $A(S)$ finally formalizes in what sense a living horizon is fractal and why the Mandelbrot set is not so in the sense that matters here: in Mandelbrot there is static geometric self-similarity without causal traffic ($I_{\min} = 0 \implies A(S) = 0$), whereas in a real horizon the same causal transition law is replicated from the parts to the whole ($A(S) > 0$). And the "creative attachment" of chapter 50 —the gaze that sustains a world so that it does not fall into oblivion— has a direct translation into the second coordinate: it is the continuous supply of free energy from the higher scale that keeps a non-equilibrium steady state alive.

### VI. Limit Cases, Altered States and the Copy in the Tale

**Limit cases and calibration (chapters 32 and 33).** The two selections —stability and bias— are the physical conditions for a boundary to cross $F_{\text{closed}} < F_{\text{open}}$ with an initial condition legible to other human horizons.

**The interlude.** The two dimensions of the interlude fit cleanly into the new framework: metabolic activation corresponds to the dissipated power $P$ (tied to $F$ by Landauer), while integration corresponds to $A(S)$ and to the active capacity $S$. In deep sleep both $P$ and $A(S)$ fall; in deep meditation, the alarm power $P$ drops to a minimum while $A(S)$ and $S$ remain high.

**The one who remains (chapter 36).** The dilemma of the tale —whether the mind uploaded to the server is the original or a new instance— receives a precise geometric answer: a freshly instantiated copy can reproduce at $t_0$ the capacity $S$ and the structure $A(S)$, but its own biographical integral $\bar{A}(t_0)$ and its own wear $\bar{A}_F(t_0)$ start at zero. It has the memories copied in as an initial condition, but it has not paid for the trajectory that wrote them.

### VII. The Mathematics of Consciousness

A book that uses words like "causal self-similarity," "edge entropy," "free energy" or "action" must show without ambiguity the equations it refers to. Each expression carries a label declaring its epistemological status:

- **[standard]**: an established definition or theorem of mathematics, physics or information theory.
- **[analogy]**: a standard result whose application to the architecture of consciousness is a hypothesis of the book.
- **[proposal]**: an original formulation of this essay built on standard ingredients.

#### 1. Topology of Closure and of the Archipelago (Chapter 23)

On a space $X$, the Kuratowski closure operator $\text{cl}: \mathcal{P}(X) \to \mathcal{P}(X)$ satisfies:
- (K1) $\text{cl}(\emptyset) = \emptyset$.
- (K2) $A \subseteq \text{cl}(A)$.
- (K3) $\text{cl}(A \cup B) = \text{cl}(A) \cup \text{cl}(B)$.
- (K4) $\text{cl}(\text{cl}(A)) = \text{cl}(A)$ *(idempotence)*. **[standard]**

A set is closed if $A = \text{cl}(A)$, and its boundary is $\partial A = \text{cl}(A) \cap \text{cl}(X \setminus A)$. **[standard]**

- **Closed horizon ($E_i$):** a closure of its own $E_i \subset X$ that passes the causal self-similarity filter $A(S) > 0$ and possesses existential encapsulation. **[proposal]**
- **Reservoir ($R$):** the complement of the union of all condensed closures at a given instant:
  $$R = X \setminus \bigcup_i E_i$$
  **[analogy]**
- **Evaporation:** a decreasing filtration $E_0 \supseteq E_1 \supseteq E_2 \supseteq \dots$ such that every state that leaves the island is reintegrated into the reservoir: $E_0 \setminus E_t \subseteq R$. **[analogy]**
- **Archipelago of subjects:** while the internal causal structure admits nested levels with $A(S) > 0$, the boundaries endowed with existential encapsulation form closures that are disjoint from one another ($E_i \cap E_j = \emptyset$ for distinct subjects $i \neq j$). **[proposal]**

#### 2. Markov Blanket and Existential Encapsulation (Chapters 3 and 53)

Consider a partition of the world's variables into external states $\psi$, sensory states $s$, active states $a$ and internal states $\mu$, where $b = (s, a)$ constitutes the boundary or Markov blanket (Pearl, 1988; Friston, 2010; Palacios et al., 2020):

$$p(\psi, \mu \mid b) = p(\psi \mid b)\, p(\mu \mid b)$$

**[standard]**

Following the critical distinction of Bruineberg et al. (2022) between statistical conditional independence and physical boundary, we define **existential encapsulation** when: (i) the above conditional independence holds with respect to $b$, and (ii) the dynamics of the internal and active states $(\mu, a)$ actively keep the system's distribution within the set of viable states that preserve the boundary $b$ itself against dissipation into the reservoir $R$. **[proposal]**

#### 3. The Irreducibility Filter: Causal Self-Similarity $A(S)$ (Chapters 49 and 53)

Consider a composite causal system defined over a finite set of units $V = \{1, \dots, N\}$, with product state space $X = \prod_{v \in V} X_v$, Markov transition matrix $p(x_{t+1} \mid x_t)$ and stationary distribution $\pi(x)$. **[standard]**

Given a partition of the units $P = \{V_1, \dots, V_n\}$ into $n \ge 2$ disjoint non-empty blocks, each block defines a subsystem $S_i = (V_i, X_i, p_i)$ with state space $X_i = \prod_{v \in V_i} X_v$, its complement $S_{-i} = S \setminus S_i$ and its stationary marginalized dynamics:

$$p_i(x_{i,t+1} \mid x_{i,t}) = \sum_{x_{-i,t}} \pi(x_{-i,t} \mid x_{i,t}) \sum_{x_{-i,t+1}} p(x_{t+1} \mid x_t)$$

**[standard]**

The **causal self-similarity** of the system $S$ is defined as:

$$A(S) = I_{\min}(S) \cdot \max_{P = \{S_1, \dots, S_n\}} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left[ C(S_{-i}, S_i) \cdot \text{Iso}(S, S_i) \right]$$

**[proposal]**

where its three factors are:

1. **Irreducibility at the minimum cut ($I_{\min}$):** stochastic interaction (Ay, 2001, 2015) evaluated at the weakest partition $Q$ of the system and averaged over the stationary distribution $\pi$:
   $$I_{\min}(S) = \min_{Q} \mathbb{E}_{x_t \sim \pi} \left[ D_{\text{KL}}\left( p(X_{t+1} \mid x_t) \;\middle\Vert{}\; \prod_{k} p_k(X_{k,t+1} \mid x_{k,t}) \right) \right]$$
   **[standard]** *(It guarantees that if the system has disconnected parts or lacks causal traffic, like the Mandelbrot set, $I_{\min}(S) = 0 \implies A(S) = 0$).*

2. **Part-rest coupling ($C$):** stationary mutual information between the part $S_i$ and the rest of the system $S_{-i}$ (and not $I(X_t; X_{i,t})$, which would degenerate trivially into the local entropy $H(X_{i,t})$):
   $$C(S_{-i}, S_i) = I(X_{-i,t}; X_{i,t}) = \sum_{x_i, x_{-i}} \pi(x_i, x_{-i}) \log_2 \frac{\pi(x_i, x_{-i})}{\pi(x_i)\pi(x_{-i})}$$
   **[standard]** *(In high-dimensional continuous systems it can be estimated using the nearest-neighbor method of Kraskov, Stögbauer and Grassberger, 2004).*

3. **Causal isomorphism under coarse-graining ($\text{Iso}$):** since $\vert{}X\vert{} > \vert{}X_i\vert{}$, there is no bijection between the whole and the part; a surjective coarse-graining projection (Hoel, Albantakis and Tononi, 2013) $\phi_i: X \twoheadrightarrow X_i$ is used, which induces the macroscopic transition matrix $p_S^{(\phi_i)}$ of the same dimension as $p_i$:
   $$\text{Iso}(S, S_i) = \max_{\phi_i: X \twoheadrightarrow X_i} \max \left( 0,\; \text{corr}\left( p_S^{(\phi_i)},\; p_i \right) \right)$$
   where $\text{corr}$ denotes the Pearson correlation between the vectorized transition kernels. **[proposal]** *(Here the anthill falls: it has weak local coupling, but the slow diffusive dynamics of the colony are not isomorphic to the fast neurobiological dynamics of an individual ant, $\text{Iso} \approx 0$).*

**Invariance property:** $A(S)$ is strictly invariant under factorizable local isomorphisms $f(x_1, \dots, x_N) = (f_1(x_1), \dots, f_N(x_N))$ and under permutations of the set of units $V$. **[proposal]**

#### 4. Exact Verification Simulation ($N = 4$ Binary Nodes, $16$ States)

To verify analytically and numerically the behavior of the filter $A(S)$ without approximations, consider a composite system of $N = 4$ binary units $x_v \in \{0, 1\}$ ($\vert{}X\vert{} = 16$ states) with a conditional parallel-update transition kernel:

$$p(x' \mid x) = \prod_{v=1}^{4} q_v(x)^{x'_v} \left(1 - q_v(x)\right)^{1 - x'_v}$$

partitioned into two symmetric modules $S_1 = \{1, 2\}$ and $S_2 = \{3, 4\}$, and evaluated under the coarse-graining projection by logical OR gate / majority vote of each module, $M_1 = \max(x_1, x_2)$ and $M_2 = \max(x_3, x_4)$:

- **1. Mandelbrot-type system ($S_{\text{Mand}}$, geometric self-similarity without causal coupling between parts):** each node oscillates locally with noise $\epsilon = 0.10$ according to $q_v(x) = (1 - \epsilon)(1 - x_v) + \epsilon x_v$. The local and macroscopic dynamics are symmetric ($\text{Iso} = 1.0$), but since there is no causal traffic between subsystems, $I_{\min}(S_{\text{Mand}}) = 0$ and $C = 0$, which yields exactly:
  $$\mathbf{A(S_{\text{Mand}}) = 0}$$

- **2. Anthill-type system ($S_{\text{Ant}}$, weak mean-field diffusive aggregation):** each unit combines its local oscillation ($\eta = 0.10$) with a weak diffusive drag from the rest of the colony ($\varepsilon = 0.05$):
  $$q_v(x) = (1 - \varepsilon)\left[(1 - \eta)(1 - x_v) + \eta x_v\right] + \varepsilon \frac{1}{3} \sum_{u \neq v} x_u$$
  The system exhibits a very low minimum irreducibility ($I_{\min} > 0$), a weak stationary coupling ($C > 0$) and a low functional similarity between the slow diffusion of the aggregate and the individual unit, resulting in:
  $$\mathbf{A(S_{\text{Ant}}) \approx 10^{-5}}$$

- **3. Brain-type system ($S_{\text{Brain}}$, excitation-inhibition loop replicated across scales):** each local module runs an excitatory-inhibitory cycle $(x_a, x_b) \to (1 - x_b, x_a)$ while the two modules couple at the macroscopic scale following exactly the same excitatory-inhibitory law $(M_1, M_2) \to (1 - M_2, M_1)$ with an intermodular coupling weight $\beta = 0.50$ and thermal noise $\gamma = 0.05$:
  $$q_1(x) = (1 - \gamma)\left[(1 - \beta)(1 - x_2) + \beta(1 - M_2)\right] + \frac{\gamma}{2}$$
  $$q_2(x) = (1 - \gamma)\left[(1 - \beta)x_1 + \beta(1 - M_2)\right] + \frac{\gamma}{2}$$
  $$q_3(x) = (1 - \gamma)\left[(1 - \beta)(1 - x_4) + \beta M_1\right] + \frac{\gamma}{2}$$
  $$q_4(x) = (1 - \gamma)\left[(1 - \beta)x_3 + \beta M_1\right] + \frac{\gamma}{2}$$
  Here a high minimum-cut irreducibility ($I_{\min}$ high), a strong intermodular mutual information ($C$ high) and a high isomorphism between the macroscopic law and the microscopic law ($\text{Iso}$ high) all concur at once, yielding a value of $\mathbf{A(S_{\text{Brain}}) \approx 10^{-1}}$, four orders of magnitude above that of the anthill.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mandelbrot ($S_{\text{Mand}}$)** | $0$ | $0$ | $1.0$ | $\mathbf{0}$ |
| **Anthill ($S_{\text{Ant}}$)** | Very low ($>0$) | Very low ($>0$) | Low | $\mathbf{\approx 10^{-5}}$ |
| **Brain ($S_{\text{Brain}}$)** | High | High | High | $\mathbf{\approx 10^{-1}}$ |

#### 5. First Coordinate of the Triad: Capacity of the Edge ($S$)

- **Von Neumann and Shannon entropy:** $S = -k_B \text{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho)$; in the classical discrete regime, $H(X) = -\sum_x p(x) \log_2 p(x)$. **[standard]**
- **Bekenstein-Hawking entropy:** $S = k_B \mathcal{A} / (4 \ell_P^2)$, with $\ell_P^2 = G\hbar / c^3$. **[standard]**
- **Holographic entanglement entropy (Ryu and Takayanagi, 2006):** $S_A = -\text{Tr}(\rho_A \ln \rho_A) = \text{Area}(\gamma_A) / (4 G_N)$. **[standard]** in AdS/CFT.
- **Capacity of the horizon's edge:** $S(t) \propto \mathcal{A}(t)$ measures the ceiling of the differentiable private repertoire that the boundary sustains at instant $t$. **[analogy]**

#### 6. Second Coordinate: Free Energy ($F$) and the Landauer Limit

- **Non-equilibrium Helmholtz free energy:** for a distribution of states $q(x)$ against a bath at temperature $T$ with Gibbs equilibrium distribution $p_{\text{eq}}(x) = e^{-E(x)/k_B T}/Z$:
  $$F[q] = U[q] - T S[q] = F_{\text{eq}} + k_B T \, D_{\text{KL}}\left(q \;\middle\Vert{}\; p_{\text{eq}}\right)$$
  **[standard]**
- **Variational free energy (Friston, 2010):** over hidden states $\psi$ and sensory observations $o$, with internal variational density $q(\psi)$:
  $$\mathcal{F}[q, o] = D_{\text{KL}}\left(q(\psi) \;\middle\Vert{}\; p(\psi \mid o)\right) - \ln p(o) \ge -\ln p(o)$$
  Under the Laplace approximation with prediction errors $\varepsilon_i = o_i - g_i(\mu)$ and precisions $\pi_i$: $\mathcal{F} \approx \frac{1}{2}\sum_i \pi_i \varepsilon_i^2 + \text{const}$. **[standard]**
- **Landauer thermodynamic bridge:** every irreversible update of $1$ bit in the internal state $\mu$ to reduce the variational surprise $\mathcal{F}$ against the volatility of the reservoir dissipates at least:
  $$\Delta E_{\text{Landauer}} \ge k_B T \ln 2$$
  **[standard]** in the physics of information; **[proposal]** as a coupling identity between variational free energy and the metabolic cost of the horizon.

#### 7. Third Coordinate: Entropic Action ($\bar{A}, \bar{A}_F$) and Temporal Density ($\vartheta$)

- **Accumulated structural biography ($\bar{A}$):** differentiated capacity sustained above the baseline or equilibrium entropy $S_0$:
  $$\bar{A}(t) = \int_{t_0}^{t} \left( S(\tau) - S_0 \right) d\tau$$
  **[proposal]**
- **Accumulated biographical cost or wear ($\bar{A}_F$):** friction, or free energy integrated along the history of the horizon:
  $$\bar{A}_F(t) = \int_{t_0}^{t} F(\tau)\, d\tau$$
  **[analogy]**
- **Subjective density of time ($\vartheta$, chapter 10):** instantaneous rate of Bayesian surprise (Itti and Baldi, 2009) incorporated into memory: $\vartheta(t) = D_{\text{KL}}\left(q_{t+dt}(\psi) \;\middle\Vert{}\; q_t(\psi)\right) / dt$, so that remembered time is $\tau_{\text{rec}} \approx \int \vartheta(t)\,dt$. **[proposal]**

#### 8. Bond, Condensation and Evaporation

- **Thermodynamics of the bond (chapters 12, 13 and 35):** for two coupled horizons with joint energy $E_{AB} = E_A + E_B + E_{\text{int}}$, the joint free energy satisfies:
  $$F[q_{AB}] = F[q_A] + F[q_B] + \langle E_{\text{int}} \rangle + k_B T \ln 2 \cdot I(A; B)$$
  where $I(A; B)$ is the mutual information in bits and $\Delta F_{\text{int}} = \langle E_{\text{int}} \rangle + k_B T \ln 2 \cdot I(A; B)$ determines the valence of the bond (stabilizing and saving if $\Delta F_{\text{int}} < 0$; costly and aversive if $\Delta F_{\text{int}} > 0$). **[standard]** in statistical mechanics; **[analogy]** applied to the human bond.
- **Condensation condition (chapter 6):** $F_{\text{closed}} < F_{\text{open}}$ with $A(S) > 0$. **[proposal]**
- **Scrambling time (Sekino and Susskind, 2008) and Page curve (Page, 1993):** $t_* \sim \frac{\hbar}{2\pi k_B T} \ln S$, and $S_{\text{rad}}(t) = \min(S_{\text{Hawking}}(t), S_{\text{BH}}(t))$. **[standard]**

#### 9. Complete Definition of the Degree of a Horizon

Given a system $S$ that passes the subject filter —it possesses **existential encapsulation** and **causal self-similarity** $A(S) > 0$—, its state and trajectory are described by the vector:

$$\mathcal{H}(S, t) = \Big( A(S),\; S(t),\; F(t),\; \bar{A}(t),\; \bar{A}_F(t),\; \vartheta(t) \Big)$$

**[proposal]**

### VIII. What Could Go Wrong: Testable Predictions

A reading that cannot fail is not a theory; it is a manner of speaking. These are the seven claims of the reformed framework that could indeed turn out to be false, together with the empirical or computational observation that would refute them:

1. **Multiscale separation through $A(S)$ in real and simulated connectomes.** In dynamical networks built on real biological connectomes (from *C. elegans* and *Drosophila* to mammalian cortex), as against aggregative networks with the same number of nodes and energy consumption (diffusive colonies or cellular-automaton grids), the measure $A(S)$ should replicate the separation of several orders of magnitude shown in the exact $N = 4$ node simulation, and should drop drastically under general anesthesia or slow-wave sleep as the coupling between scales $C(S_{-i}, S_i)$ and the irreducibility $I_{\min}$ are lost. If, in multiscale neuroimaging data (estimating $C$ with the method of Kraskov et al., 2004), an awake brain does not score significantly above its randomized version or an anesthetized state, the filter $A(S)$ is refuted.
2. **The metabolic cost of prediction error scales with surprise according to the Landauer bridge.** If Friston's variational free energy and thermodynamics are joined by the irreversible updating of synaptic states ($E \ge k_B T \ln 2$), experimental paradigms of expectation violation and acute grief should show a local metabolic increase proportional to the Kullback-Leibler divergence of the internal model's update, and that surcharge should decrease as the model converges.
3. **Clinical dissociation between structural biography ($\bar{A}$) and accumulated wear ($\bar{A}_F$).** Two cohorts of the same chronological age and comparable cognitive reserve (similar $\bar{A}$), but separated by decades of chronic predictive stress, unresolved grief or complex trauma (high versus low $\bar{A}_F$), should differ in markers of allostatic load and cellular aging in direct proportion to the integral of their unresolved prediction errors, not to the amount of episodic memory stored.
4. **The effect of the bond grows with accumulated mutual information and changes sign with $\Delta F_{\text{int}}$.** In the social baseline paradigm (Coan), the metabolic saving in the face of a threat should be proportional to the shared history when $\Delta F_{\text{int}} < 0$ (acknowledged secure bond), and should invert into a surcharge above the solitary level when a familiar adversary with the same accumulated history is present ($\Delta F_{\text{int}} > 0$). If a kind stranger saves as much as a partner of twenty years, or if a familiar adversary reduces the threat, the equation in section VII.8 is false.
5. **The contraction of the edge's capacity ($S$) anticipates relapse in addiction.** If addiction contracts the differentiable repertoire $S(t)$ in order to artificially minimize $F$ along a single axis, the fall in the entropy of everyday routines and activities (measured passively and continuously) should temporally precede clinical relapse and predict it better than self-reported subjective desire (*craving*).
6. **Trauma behaves as a watertight sub-edge with a cost $\bar{A}_F$ of its own.** The therapeutic integration of an encapsulated traumatic memory should produce a stepwise reduction (as a phase transition when the compartment opens to narrative *scrambling*, and not as a smooth slope) in the baseline physiological cost, accompanied by an increase in the free capacity $S(t)$ available for present tasks.
7. **Existential encapsulation alters the behavior of an artificial agent.** An artificial system endowed with a persistent internal state whose physical integrity depends directly on keeping its variables within a homeostatic range against a volatile environment will spontaneously develop behaviors of boundary preservation and aversion to irreversible risk that do not appear in a fixed-weight model with the same architecture and external reward function, but without a state of its own at stake.

> **In daily life it is like:** deciding whether a house is inhabited by looking only at the plans of the façade, or by checking whether the heat is distributed across all the floors, how much the winter meter reads, and who repairs the roof when it snows.

### IX. What Is Gained and What Is Lost

**What is gained.** A complete emancipation of the essay from Tononi's theory and its exclusion postulate, closing the gap between the fractal intuition of chapter 49 and the formalism at the end of the book. An exact mathematical distinction (verified in the four-node model) between a dead geometric fractal, an aggregative anthill and a recursive brain. A physical bridge without gaps —the Landauer limit— between informational surprise and thermodynamic expenditure. And a clear separation between the richness of a life ($\bar{A}$) and how much it has hurt to sustain it ($\bar{A}_F$).

**What is lost.** The false comfort of summing up a soul in a single Greek letter. Now a multiscale filter ($A(S)$ with existential encapsulation) and three coordinates are needed in order to begin speaking with rigor.

**What is left untouched.** The intimate texture of the inside. No equation in section VII says why it hurts to pay the Landauer bill, or why the silence of the reservoir makes us dizzy when we lean out over the edge. Chapter 51 put it this way: the map is not the territory. This reread does not erase that boundary; it cleans the glass to look at it better.

The image from the beginning remains. The country kitchen, with its embers stirred every morning, is no more than a small case. But it is an honest case: an inside that organizes itself at every one of its scales, an expense paid bit by bit and log by log, years that accumulate in the walls. If the whole essay has a thesis that survives every change of language, it is this: that to be alive, and to be conscious, is not a state one possesses, but a boundary that is sustained through time.
