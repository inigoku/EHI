To consolidate the intuitions of the first part of the book, it helps to draw a comparative map. This experiment does not propose loose metaphors: it suggests that consciousness shares an **informational topology** with other structures of the physical, logical and spiritual universe.

Encapsulation is not exclusive to software, evaporation does not belong only to the thermodynamics of black holes, and emptiness is not a concept reserved for Eastern mystics. All these languages describe, at different scales, the same phase transitions and the same limits of access.

The following table serves as the book's Rosetta Stone: it translates the central concepts of the horizon into their equivalents in three other domains:

| Dimension | Philosophy of the horizon | Software engineering | Black hole physics | Eastern philosophies |
| **Origin / emptiness** | The reservoir (pure potential of unstructured information). | The *heap* (unallocated global dynamic memory, a common reserve). | Quantum vacuum / underlying field and fluctuations. | *Hun Dun* (primordial chaos, Taoism) / *shunyata* (emptiness, Buddhism) / *Brahman nirguna* (Hinduism). |
| **Birth / identity** | Condensation (establishing the circular boundary of the self). | Instantiation and encapsulation (creating the object, `private` variables). | Gravitational collapse / formation of the event horizon (Schwarzschild). | The seven openings of *Hun Dun* / *de*, the minimal efficacy that survives collapse (Taoism) / *maya* / *Brahman saguna* (Hinduism). |
| **Subjective experience** | Private state (first-person perspective, accessible from within). | Internal state (`private fields` hidden behind the interface). | Region inside the horizon (inaccessible to an outside observer). | *Atman* (the self, Hinduism) versus the bundle of perceptions without a self (Hume, Buddhist *anattā*). |

### The nature of the asymmetry

The value of this table lies not in how curious its parallels are, but in what it reveals about the structure of reality. If the "hard problem" of consciousness seems unsolvable, it is because we usually approach it from a flat ontology: we assume that everything that exists must be publicly observable.

The modeling of complex systems and the physics of horizons show that this is not so: the universe is asymmetric. Building complex systems, whether computer programs, event horizons or brains, requires, by thermodynamic and logical necessity, drawing boundaries that hide information. Subjectivity is the inevitable perspective of any domain that has managed to close itself enough to harbor a private state.

> **Note to Chapter 9**
>
> **What we do know:** That encapsulation is a general principle of system design and that event horizons define boundaries of causal access. That both are the same structure in the case of consciousness is this book's hypothesis, not a fact.
>
> **What we don't know:** Whether this structural agreement points to a unified informational law underlying physics, computation and mind, or whether it reflects the limit of the explanatory capacity of our own brain.
>
> **If you take away only one idea:** The self is not a substance but a topology of access. There is no "ghost in the machine"; there is a private state protected by the same kind of boundary that nature and engineers use to keep systems from sinking into chaos.
>
