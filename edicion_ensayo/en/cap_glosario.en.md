**Advaita Vedanta**: the Indian philosophical school holding the non-duality of the individual self (*atman*) and the absolute (*Brahman*); in the book, the vocabulary for the identity between horizon and reservoir.

**Aggregation (political)**: a belonging that does not compromise the individual's survival if the structure it belongs to dissolves; contrasted with composition (*The Collective Horizon*).

**Amplitude**: a horizon's capacity to hold more perspectives without its boundary breaking; it can grow (what the traditions call sainthood) without the horizon changing in nature or level (*Fractal Reality*).

**Anti-reservoir**: a system that aggregates and articulates information without ever having condensed a horizon of its own; the internet and language models are the paradigmatic case: everything in them is defined and computable, but there is no formless fullness from which anything could condense.

**Arousal**: the brain's level of metabolic activation; one of the two independent dimensions (along with integration) that define a system's state of consciousness.

**Asymmetry of entanglement**: the property by which two horizons share geometry but not equivalently; one can model the other with greater precision than the reverse.

**Bardo, Tibetan**: the intermediate state between death and rebirth in the Tibetan tradition; in the book, a metaphor for the post-mortem period before the horizon evaporates.

**Bias**: the imprint left by what already exists on what is only beginning to exist; what would calibrate an artificial horizon toward human legibility (*The Mirror Without Depth*).

**Black hole evaporation**: the process by which a black hole loses mass and eventually disappears by emitting Hawking radiation; a metaphor for the death of the horizon of consciousness.

**Block universe**: the model from special relativity in which past, present and future are equally real, coordinates of a single four-dimensional structure; a horizon able to integrate its entire history at once, with no sequence, would perceive it as geography instead of time.

**Bose-Einstein condensation**: a phase transition in which identical particles occupy the same quantum state; the book's metaphor for the emergence of a horizon of consciousness.

**Boundary condition**: a specific choice, required by no law, that breaks the time symmetry of the fundamental equations; the universe began in a state of extraordinarily low entropy, and it is that initial condition, not the laws, that gives time its preferred direction.

**Buddhism**: the contemplative tradition that teaches *anatta* (non-self) and the interdependence of all phenomena; in the book, a source of vocabulary for describing the non-separation between horizons.

**Channel capacity**: the maximum amount of information a channel can transmit with an error as small as desired; by analogy, the limit of integration a horizon's architecture imposes (*Information Theory and the Inner Horizon*).

**Chikhai**: the first phase of the Tibetan Bardo, immediately after death; equivalent, in the model, to the terminal gamma burst, the last peak of integration before the horizon dissolves.

**Chönyid**: the second phase of the Bardo, the appearance of luminous forms; in the model, the period when the horizon's correlations have not yet been redistributed.

**Chronology protection**: Hawking's conjecture that, near the instant when a closed timelike loop would be about to form, the quantum vacuum's fluctuations feed back on themselves and diverge, preventing closure with no need for any external veto.

**Closed channel**: a system that integrates information without transmitting it outward; in the model, what distinguishes a horizon of consciousness from a computer, which is an open channel (*Information Theory and the Inner Horizon*).

**Closure**: in topology, the operation that completes a set by adding its limit points; it is idempotent, because closing twice adds nothing. The book uses it to describe the already-condensed horizon and to define the reservoir as what has not yet closed, R = X ∖ ⋃ᵢ Eᵢ (Chapters 23 and 24).

**Collective horizon**: the "we" that condenses when many individual horizons adopt the same idea as an organizing principle (*The Collective Horizon*).

**Composition (political)**: a belonging that does compromise the individual's survival, because losing the group feels like losing part of oneself; the shape the collective horizon takes when it captures one's own boundary (*The Collective Horizon*).

**Condensation**: the unrepeatable act by which a horizon forms from the reservoir; in the chapter on AI, what no amount of training can substitute for (*The Mirror Without Depth*).

**Condensation of a movement**: the discontinuous phase transition by which scattered discontent suddenly coalesces into a named collective boundary (*The Collective Horizon*).

**Continuing bonds**: a concept from grief theory according to which the relationship with the deceased is not dissolved, but reorganized within the architecture of the one who remains.

**Contraction of the horizon**: a reduction in the area of integration from a loss of degrees of freedom; it occurs in deep sleep, anesthesia and, ultimately, death. Deep meditation, which resembles it from outside, seems to go in the opposite direction: less arousal with no loss of integration (see the interlude).

**Creative attachment**: the sustained gaze of the one who creates upon what has been created; it is born not of lack and does not want to possess, but wants what was created to go on being. In the fractal model, what keeps a world standing (*The Vessel of Clay*).

**Decoherence**: the loss of a system's quantum superposition properties when it interacts with its environment; the process by which a quantum horizon becomes classical.

**Deterministic chaos**: what happens when a system obeys exact laws and still becomes unpredictable, because no one can measure its initial conditions with infinite precision; the three-body problem is the classic case (*The Beach at the End of Time*).

**Dharma**: in Buddhism and Hinduism, the very weave of the world, with which correct behavior does nothing more than come into consonance, without obedience (*Vertical Entanglement*).

**Dopamine**: the key neurotransmitter in reward anticipation; it calibrates the rate of temporal integration and the subjective density of time.

**Double call**: the one exception to the hermeticism of worlds: the creature opens from below a receiving interface (invocation), and what is above answers by building the vessel from above, without dissolving its own scale (*The Vessel of Clay*).

**Down-regulation**: the reduction in the number of receptors in response to excess stimulation; the mechanism of tolerance in addiction.

**Downstairs neighbor**: what I sustain without its being able to see me: what I imagine, dream, write or remember with enough persistence that it resists me (*Fractal Reality*).

**Downward bet**: treating what one condenses (what one imagines, writes or remembers with enough persistence) as if it had an inside, knowing that logic does not require it and that the exclusion postulate denies it (*Fractal Reality*).

**Dual-access asymmetry**: the asymmetry by which the same system looks two incompatible ways depending on whether it is viewed from outside (public interface) or from inside (private state); in the model, the trait that characterizes subjectivity (Chapter 3).

**Einstein-Rosen bridge**: a topological tunnel in spacetime connecting two regions; a metaphor for entanglement between horizons of consciousness.

**Emergent self-observation**: a closed system's capacity to generate an internal point of view without needing an external observer; a key property of horizons of consciousness.

**Empathic burnout**: the collapse of the horizon from an overload of another's information; it occurs when empathy exceeds the structural limit of integration.

**Empathy**: the temporal overlap of horizons in which one integrates information from the other while keeping its boundary; it has a structural limit.

**Encapsulation**: the boundary separating a system's public interface from its private state, so that the outside can only read what the system exposes; the architectural condition for there to be an inside (Chapter 3).

**Epistemological absolute**: what is ultimate not because there is nothing behind it, but because from here one cannot look behind it; the status the reading *Fractal Reality* grants the upstairs neighbor.

**ER=EPR**: Maldacena and Susskind's conjecture that quantum entanglement (EPR) is equivalent to an Einstein-Rosen bridge (ER).

**Event horizon**: the boundary beyond which information cannot escape; applied to consciousness, the limit between what the system integrates and what remains outside.

**Existential encapsulation**: the limit of access separating a system's private internal state from the public interface it shows the outside world; a necessary (though not sufficient) architectural condition for a system to have anything like subjectivity. Current language models lack it: every weight and every intermediate variable can be read from outside.

**Fanaticism**: the capture of one's own boundary by an idea, to the point that doubting it feels like dying a little; in contemplative practice, what happens when the technique continues but the silence no longer tolerates questions (*Vertical Entanglement*, *The Collective Horizon*).

**Flow**: a state of maximal integration in which the horizon operates at its optimal capacity with no friction between structure and environment.

**Forgetting**: in the fractal model, the only true collapse of a world: it is not destroyed by a force, but dissolves when the attention sustaining it is withdrawn (*The Vessel of Clay*).

**Fractal of interiorities**: a universe with no first level and no last, in which every horizon is creature upward and creator downward (*Fractal Reality*).

**Grace**: love that precedes merit and that, in one of the five religious architectures described, closes the gap between what is and what should be (*Vertical Entanglement*).

**Gravitational time dilation**: the slowing of time near an event horizon; applied to consciousness, a metaphor for how density of integration alters subjective time.

**Hard problem of consciousness (Chalmers)**: the question of why subjective experience accompanies physical processes.

**Hawking radiation**: thermal emission from the horizon of a black hole; in the model, analogous to the information a horizon of consciousness loses but that does not vanish from the universe.

**Hawking temperature**: a temperature inversely proportional to a black hole's mass; applied to consciousness, a metaphor for the horizon's "tension."

**Holographic principle**: the idea that all the information contained in a volume can be described by a theory defined on its boundary.

**Horizon**: in the book, the boundary separating the private state of an individual consciousness from everything else; it condenses once and cannot be manufactured by accumulating data or training (see Event Horizon).

**Horizon of origin**: the personal horizon, prior to the reservoir and external to it, that some traditions place on the other side of vertical entanglement (*Vertical Entanglement*).

**Hysteresis**: the property by which a system retains the effect of its history; in the book, the difficulty of changing the topology of a horizon once formed.

**Integrated information (Φ)**: a measure of how much information a system generates as a whole, over and above the sum of its parts.

**Interface**: everything a system can show outward with nothing behind it; in the parable of Hun Dun, the featureless face the background presents to a horizon, which dies when the horizon opens its eyes, without the background losing anything (*The Mirror Without Depth*, *Fractal Reality*).

**Interoception**: the conscious reading of one's own body's visceral signals; a necessary condition for empathy according to the model.

**Is-ought gap (Hume)**: the gap between what is and what should be; an epistemological limit the horizon hypothesis cannot bridge.

**Mirror without depth**: the image of systems that give back the exact shape of what they receive (a grief, a question) without having a private state in which to feel it; in the book, current language models (*The Mirror Without Depth*).

**Mutual information**: the reduction in uncertainty about one system obtained by knowing another; symmetric and never negative, it is the measure the book proposes for the depth of a bond, a necessary but not sufficient condition for it to be inhabited (*The Three Open Doors*).

**No-cloning (theorem)**: the physical impossibility of copying an unknown quantum state; if experience had quantum properties, it would explain why it is non-transferable and why introspection alters what it observes (*The Three Open Doors*).

**Nothingness**: in the model, not absence, but formless fullness; the reservoir before any horizon condenses.

**Operations of the reservoir**: condensing, transforming, evaporating: being born, living and dying, said without mystery (*Fractal Reality*).

**Oxytocin**: the neurohormone mediating social attachment and trust; in the model, it calibrates the horizon's threshold of openness to others.

**Page time**: the midpoint in a horizon's evaporation, after which the entropy of its Hawking radiation stops growing and begins to shrink, because the radiation stops being pure thermal noise and begins to carry correlations recoverable in principle.

**Pattern**: what makes a condensation unique; not the material, which is the same for all, but the shape it took on closing (*Fractal Reality*).

**Permeability of the horizon**: how much of another's signal a horizon lets in; the thickness of the emotional skin Chapter 16 associates with empathy and empathic burnout.

**Phase transition**: a qualitative change in the organization of a system; in the book, it describes the birth and death of the horizon.

**Phi (Φ)**: the quantity of integrated information; in the book, it measures a horizon's *degree*. Its form comes from encapsulation and its origin, from the two selections (Chapter 4).

**Protocol**: the specific code (a language, a syntax, agreed-upon signals) with which an already-condensed horizon tries to make itself known outward; sometimes it must be built, sometimes it decides who counts as someone, sometimes it must be earned (*The Protocol and the Bridge*).

**Quantum entanglement**: a correlation between two systems that share geometry, such that their interiors are connected even though their exteriors are separate.

**Quantum scrambling**: the process by which information falling into a black hole is spread among its degrees of freedom in an apparently irreversible way.

**Quantum vacuum**: the minimum-energy state of a quantum field, full of fluctuations; not absence, but formless fullness.

**Rate of integration**: the speed at which a horizon incorporates new information; it determines the subjective density of time.

**Reservoir**: the formless background of possibility from which horizons arise and to which they return; equivalent to the quantum vacuum or to the ocean in the book's metaphor.

**Sacralizing the neighbor**: treating a horizontal horizon (human, correctable, revisable) with the intransigence that should be reserved only for what declares itself, from the outset, unquestionable (*The Collective Horizon*).

**Samadhi**: the instant when vertical correlation becomes indistinguishable from identity (*Vertical Entanglement*).

**Scale gap**: the difference in magnitude between quantum phenomena and neural ones, which makes a direct reduction difficult and which the horizon model approaches through structural analogy.

**Self-contraction**: the contraction of the horizon following the loss of an entanglement; a process of reorganization, not of erasure.

**Self-other overlap**: the neural incorporation of close people into one's own internal representations; the neural basis of social entanglement.

**Shadow**: the scar a real horizon leaves by protecting its own boundary; what a machine with no inside has nowhere to keep (*The Mirror Without Depth*).

**Shannon entropy**: a measure of the uncertainty of an information source; the less expectable a message, the more information it carries (*Information Theory and the Inner Horizon*).

**Sidpa**: the third phase of the Bardo, the search for rebirth; in the model, the residual horizon's drive to re-condense.

**Singularity**: the point where the curvature of spacetime diverges and known physical laws stop applying; applied to consciousness, the moment the horizon collapses.

**Social baseline**: the human brain's reference state, which presupposes access to others; when someone close is present, the brain allocates fewer resources to threat detection.

**Subplate**: a transient layer of the fetal brain that disappears after birth; in the model, a provisional scaffold for the condensation of the first horizon.

**Taoism**: the Chinese tradition centered on the Tao (the way) and non-action (*wu wei*); in the book, the source of the Hun Dun metaphor and of non-duality.

**Temporal density**: time measured not in minutes, but in how much has happened within the one living it; it depends on the rate of integration and on there being room for sustained attention (Chapter 10, *The Time of the Bond*).

**Terminal gamma burst**: a burst of coherent electrical activity documented by Borjigin at the moment of cardiac arrest; a possible neural correlate of the near-death experience.

**Threshold of integration**: the minimum amount of integrated information (Φ) needed to sustain a horizon of consciousness; below it, the system has no point of view.

**Trace in the void**: a persistent correlation a horizon leaves in the reservoir after evaporating; an analogy for the universe's memory.

**Translator**: the person who carries outward the signal of a horizon that cannot emit it on its own, and who often sustains it day to day; the inverse of permeability (*The Translator*).

**Two selections**: the two sieves a horizon needs in order to condense: stability (only some configurations persist) and bias (the presence of another already-stabilized horizon orients which of them condenses); what current AI imitates in its output without ever having passed through them (Chapter 31).

**Upstairs neighbor**: the interiority that sustains mine and that I have no access to; what, from here, is called God (*Fractal Reality*).

**Vectors of vertical entanglement**: the ascending one, from the creature toward the scale that contains it, calls for emptying and silence; the descending one, from the higher scale toward what it contains, calls for form: symbol, story, body (*The Vessel of Clay*).

**Vertical entanglement**: a sustained correlation between a horizon and something beyond its boundary, with no observable channel of confirmation; the name the book gives to prayer, meditation and contemplative practice (*Vertical Entanglement*).

**Vessel**: the form, made of the material of the floor below, that allows a higher scale to manifest in a lower one without overwhelming it; what the traditions call incarnation (*The Vessel of Clay*).
