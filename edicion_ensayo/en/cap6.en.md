> *The water came back at night. Without warning. Without signs. In the morning the inhabitants found the shore where it had always been. The same mud. The same stones. The same line of salt on the low walls. No one could say whether the water had brought anything with it. No one could say whether it had left anything behind.*

In 1974 Stephen Hawking discovered that black holes are not eternal. Nothing destroys them from outside: the quantum vacuum, disturbed by the horizon, generates a constant flow of radiation that slowly drains their mass. The temperature of that radiation is inversely proportional to the mass: the smaller the black hole, the hotter and brighter it is. Evaporation accelerates itself.

---

### The vacuum that is not empty

To understand why black holes evaporate, you have to let go of a deep-rooted intuition: that the vacuum is absence. The quantum vacuum is not empty. It is an ocean of possibilities, of fluctuations that appear and vanish so fast that we cannot detect them directly. Virtual particles, pairs of matter and antimatter, arise from the vacuum, live for a fraction of a second and annihilate each other before the universe notices they existed.

A black hole disturbs this dance. Near the horizon, gravity is so extreme that it can separate a virtual pair before it annihilates: one particle falls in and the other escapes out. The one that escapes is Hawking radiation. The one that falls carries negative energy with it, because total energy must be conserved, and that is why it reduces the mass of the hole.

The paradox is that the black hole radiates not because it has internal heat, but because the vacuum itself, seen from its gravity, has a temperature. The horizon is a causal boundary, and that boundary, interacting with the quantum vacuum, produces radiation the way red-hot iron produces steam when it gets wet.

> **In physics this is called:** a Hawking temperature proportional to the surface gravity of the horizon.

### The information paradox

In 1976 Hawking published a paper that dismayed the physics community. He argued that when a black hole evaporates completely, the information that fell into it is lost forever. Not that it becomes hard to recover: it ceases to exist as information. The universe goes from a pure state, in which everything is known, to a mixed state, in which only probabilities are known.

This violates one of the most sacred principles of quantum physics: unitarity. In quantum mechanics, information is neither created nor destroyed: it is transformed. If Hawking was right, quantum physics was broken at its core.

For almost forty years this paradox was the most famous problem in theoretical physics. Radical solutions were proposed: that the information escaped in some hidden way, that the black hole left a remnant that contained it, or that quantum mechanics had to be modified. The community split into camps, and that information war was, in many ways, a war for the soul of physics.

> **In physics this is called:** the black hole information paradox.

The solution that seems most likely today, although it is not proven, is that information is not destroyed when the horizon disappears but undergoes a process of *scrambling*: it disperses and becomes irreversibly entangled with the outside environment, losing its local coherence while remaining globally conserved in the physics of the reservoir.

### In its last fractions of a second

In its last fractions of a second, the temperature soars. The horizon shrinks and the boundary between inside and outside grows fainter and fainter, until, in the Planck regime, semiclassical physics no longer holds and what happens next is simply unknown.

What we do know is that there is an instant when the horizon ceases to exist. It does not contract to a point: it disappears. The boundary that separated inside from outside stops being one, and with it the interior disappears.

Three things may remain afterwards:

1. **A Planck remnant**: a microscopic object that would contain all the information of the horizon compressed into its internal states.
2. **An imprint in the vacuum**: what Hawking, Perry and Strominger called *soft hair*, permanent deformations of the quantum field that record everything that crossed the horizon, not as an object but as a texture of the vacuum.
3. **Nothing**: complete evaporation and total reintegration into the field, with no localizable residue.

### The debate that death raises

Hawking's original answer was brutal: yes, the information is lost; the horizon acts as a cosmic incinerator.

Most physicists rejected that conclusion. Unitarity (information cannot be destroyed, only transformed) is too central to abandon. In recent decades the balance has tipped toward conservation.

The most elegant mechanism is ER=EPR: the particles produced by evaporation are not simply radiated outwards but remain entangled with the interior of the horizon through Einstein-Rosen bridges, microscopic geometric connections that make the outside and the inside, in a deep sense, the same system described from two perspectives.

The practical result is **scrambling**: the information the horizon contained mixes progressively and irreversibly with the outside field.

> **In physics this is called:** quantum scrambling.

The information exists, but in practice it cannot be recovered. The reservoir remembers, but cannot tell anyone.

### The near-death experience

There is a phenomenon that physics has not explained and that medicine is only beginning to study seriously: near-death experiences. They are not random hallucinations; they have a surprisingly constant structure across cultures, ages and clinical conditions.

The pattern is recognizable: a feeling of deep peace, the experience of leaving the body and watching it from above, passage through a dark tunnel toward a bright light, an encounter with beings of light or with people who have died, a review of one's own life (not as judgment but as understanding) and, finally, a return to the body, often with reluctance.

Skeptics attribute it all to cerebral anoxia, to a dysregulated visual cortex or to a massive release of endogenous dimethyltryptamine at the moment of collapse. And it is true that many of these phenomena can be reproduced artificially: pilots subjected to high G-forces in a centrifuge describe tunnels of light, and patients whose temporal lobe is stimulated electrically report sensing presences.

But some details do not fit. There are accounts of patients with minimal brain activity, with a flat electroencephalogram, who describe complex, coherent experiences, with details that could only be checked afterwards: conversations that took place in the room while they were clinically dead, things they could not have seen from where they lay, objects placed on high shelves out of sight of the bed. It must be said just as clearly that these are anecdotal accounts: the AWARE study, which placed hidden images on high shelves in resuscitation rooms precisely to test them, has so far recorded no verified case of anyone seeing them.

We do not know what these experiences are, nor whether they are elaborate illusions produced by a dying brain or something more. What we do know is that their phenomenology, what people describe, matches strikingly the contemplative cartography of the *Bardo Thödol*, composed centuries ago in Tibet.

### The bardo: the interval between horizons

The *Bardo Thödol*, the Tibetan text known as the *Book of the Dead*, describes with surprising precision what happens between the dissolution of the horizon and its complete return to the field. Not as a map of the afterlife in a religious sense, but as a phenomenology of the process of disappearing.

- **Chikhai**: the instant of complete evaporation. The primordial Clear Light appears, the nature of the field without a horizon, the vacuum seen from within. Whoever recognizes it as their own nature is liberated directly; most, with their usual mind, do not recognize what they see and fade before the immensity.

- **Chönyid**: scrambling under way, with the correlations of the dissolved horizon still semi-coherent, before they are fully redistributed. Visions of peaceful and wrathful deities appear, which can be read as modes of the quantum field carrying fragments of the horizon's information, still recognizable before scrambling is complete.

- **Sidpa**: scrambling complete, with the information spread through the field in search of conditions to condense again. Karmic attraction, the resonance of a pattern of tendencies with compatible conditions, is the contemplative image of what physics calls the conditions for a new phase transition.

This correspondence is a reading the experiment proposes; it is foreign to the traditional interpretation of the *Bardo Thödol*.

What matters is that the three states of the bardo have concrete counterparts in documented phenomena: the Clear Light recalls the burst of coherent gamma activity that Jimo Borjigin's team recorded in brains in cardiac arrest (we will return to it in the interlude); the visions of the intermediate bardo, hypnagogic hallucinations and near-death experiences; and the recondensation of Sidpa, the physical idea that scrambled information remains in the field as a condition for future condensations.

### Three answers to the same question

Did anything of the city come back with the water?

**Advaita Vedanta**: the city never stopped being water. The jīva, the individual consciousness, was Brahman with superimposed limits, and those limits were māyā: its separation was only apparent. The wave is a pattern of movement in a continuous medium. When it breaks there is no return: an apparent localization simply ceases. Nothing of the city came back because the city was the water all along.

**Taoism**: the city was a name. Zhuangzi would not say that those correlations remain in the field. He would say the question assumes the city was something more than water. Chapter 16 of the *Tao Te Ching* says it: all things return to their root, and that return is called stillness. No memory, no correlation, no imprint. To die is to return to one's destiny, which consists in having no destiny.

**Buddhism**: something came back, but no longer as a city. The doctrine of anattā, non-self, rejects both eternalism and annihilationism. There is no substance that transmigrates, but there is no complete dissolution either. What continues is karma, which is not a moral debt but a causal pattern without an owner. The flame of one candle lights another, and the second is not the first, but neither is it something entirely different: there is causal continuity without substantial identity.

### The wound that heals

There is a way of telling this whole chapter that needs no equations. I owe it to Gerard, who was thirteen when I first heard it from him.

I had explained the full cycle to him: how a star, when it runs out of fuel, collapses in on itself in a matter of seconds (the core crushing, the outer layers falling at a fraction of the speed of light, the supernova that for a few weeks outshines its whole galaxy) and how, afterwards, the resulting black hole takes eons to evaporate: an almost imperceptible trickle of radiation that, for a black hole of stellar mass, lasts far longer than the universe has existed.

Gerard thought about it and said something that I, in years of writing about this, had not managed to put so cleanly: that the birth of the horizon was a wound, something that opens suddenly and violently in the smooth fabric of the vacuum, and that evaporation was the way that wound had of healing. Slowly, silently, with no one noticing from outside, until one day there is nothing left to heal.

He did not know the *Tao Te Ching*, had not read Zhuangzi or heard of Hun Dun. And yet he had arrived on his own at the same image that chapter 5 borrowed from Taoism: the reservoir as an unwounded fullness, and the horizon as the cut that fullness has to suffer in order, for a while, to be someone. It is like a wound and its scar: opening takes an instant; closing, a whole life.

There is a nuance worth adding to his image, not to correct it but to complete it. A wound that truly heals almost never leaves the skin as it was before it opened. A mark remains, paler, finer, but there. And that, almost to the letter, is what physics says happens to the reservoir.

### Karma as the geometry of the reservoir

What the traditions name in different ways (saṃskāra, karma) points to the same structure: the information the horizon imprinted on the field while it existed does not disappear with it. It does not endure as identity, or as memory, or in the hands of anyone who owns it, but as a condition, like the shape the wind left on the water before it calmed.

Karma is the configuration of the reservoir at the moment a new horizon condenses. The correlations left by the previous one are still there, scrambled, dispersed, nameless, and they condition the particular shape the next condensation will take. No soul transmigrates: the reservoir that receives the return is no longer the same one that existed before that horizon arose. Every horizon changes the field it arises from and returns to.

The physical part is a direct consequence of the conservation of information; calling it karma is already interpretation. If information is not destroyed, the configuration of the field after evaporation is different from what it was before the horizon formed. That difference is, in the language of the experiment, karma.

### What the question reveals

On the substantial level (did the city come back as an entity?), all the traditions say no. There is no remnant with an identity and no self that persists.

On the causal level (did the city influence the water that came back?), they all say yes. The field after evaporation is not identical to the one before: there is an imprint, though a nameless one.

On the informational level (does the water carry the city within it?), the answers diverge. Vedanta and Maldacena say yes, in the form of non-local correlations. Taoism and the early Hawking say no, or that the question is badly posed. Buddhism says yes, but scrambled.

What the question reveals, once asked, is that we want the city to have come back. Grief proves that the question matters more than the answer.

### The closing of the cycle

The inhabitants found the shore exactly where it had been. The same mud, the same stones. There were no signs that the water had taken anything or left anything.

But the water was not the same. The quantum field in which its molecules move had been altered in ways no instrument can fully measure. The correlations were there, present in the mathematics and inaccessible in practice.

The city did not come back, and the water that came back was not new. The horizon evaporated, and the reservoir, altered, received what the horizon had been, not as memory, but as an invisible texture of the field.

This does not console, nor does it try to. Physics does not promise immortality, and neither does this experiment. What it tries to offer is precision: a more faithful map of the territory.

The horizon arose from the reservoir and returned to it. Between those two instants there was someone who loved, remembered, lost and built. The fact that this someone can no longer be located in the field does not erase that they were there. The fact that the reservoir cannot tell what it remembers does not mean it does not remember.

The experiment has not yet answered whether anything of the city came back with the water.

Perhaps that is the answer.

> **Note to Chapter 7**
>
> **What we do know:** Hawking predicted that black holes emit radiation and evaporate (the prediction is solid, although no one has yet observed it). Information, according to most physicists, is conserved (unitarity). Gamma bursts after cardiac arrest have been recorded in rats (Borjigin et al., 2013) and in two of four dying patients (Xu et al., 2023). Near-death experiences have a very similar phenomenology across cultures, although with documented cultural variations.
>
> **What we don't know:** What happens in the Planck regime. Whether the information is really recoverable. Whether the Bardo describes anything more than phenomenology. Whether near-death experiences are brain illusions or something deeper.
>
> **Open questions:** Is karma "the geometry of the reservoir" or an inadmissible reductionism? Can physics speak of the "interval" without coloring it with a particular culture? Why are the near-death experiences of people from different cultures so alike?
>
> **If you take away only one idea:** Dying is not switching off but evaporating: the boundary dissolves and the interior returns to the field. What you were does not disappear; it mixes. And the field, altered, is never exactly the same again.
>
